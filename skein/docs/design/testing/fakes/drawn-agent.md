# A drawn agent: what the fake model says when no script answers

Provisional, 2026-11-27. When no script answers a request, skein's fake LLM (`skein-fake-llm-domain`, [llm.md](llm.md)) draws the answer from the application's menu: a drawn agent. This document says how a menu body names a value the conversation holds, how each answer is drawn by its request, apart from the fake's faults and timing, and how a drawn agent is captured and replayed. Written from `temper/docs/design/draft/misbehaving-agents.md`, 4.2 to 4.4.

## 1. In one page

- **Slots** (section 2): a menu body may hold a slot, a placeholder the fake fills as it draws the call, with a value it finds between two delimiters in what the conversation holds, or with the slot's own value that the world never issues. The fake knows only the delimiters: no tool's name, meaning or lifetime. The world classifies what was drawn.
- **Named draws** (section 3): each answer is drawn from the agent's own seed, keyed by its conversation, its logical turn and its request's attempt. The fake's failures and its timing draw from seeds of their own, so a fault changes which requests are made, never what one of them draws.
- **The capture** (section 4): each drawn answer is recorded by its key, with its slots' draws and the world's classes beside it. Played back, the capture answers each request by the same key, while faults and timing come from the case's seeds.

## 2. Slots

- **Declared by the menu,** beside its bodies: a slot has a name, a `before` and an `after`, the value the world never issues, a weight, and, for a numeric slot, its bounds. A menu holds at most 8 slots, their names distinct, each 1 to 32 bytes of ASCII letters, digits and `_`; `before`, `after` and the never-issued value are each 1 to 256 bytes, the last with no line feed, and a number for a numeric slot (1 to 20 ASCII digits, with no leading zero but `0` alone). The weight is per mille, at most 1000, and a numeric slot's bounds are inclusive, low at most high. Slots share `script_bytes` with the scripts and the rest of the menu. A slot that breaks a rule is refused at configuration, as a script is.
- **Used by a body as a take's value is** (llm.md, 2.6): `"{{#NAME}}"`, its quotes included, by the value as a JSON number, for a numeric slot alone; `{{NAME}}` anywhere else by the value escaped as a JSON string's content, `"` and `\` after a backslash and each byte below 0x20 as `\u00XX`. A `"{{#NAME}}"` naming a slot that isn't numeric is refused at configuration; a placeholder naming no slot stays as written. The menu's invalid entries hold no slot: they are copied as written.
- **Where it looks:** in what a take reads, in its order (llm.md, 2.6): the query's messages from the last to the first, each one's parts from the last to the first, each text part's text and each tool output's output, then the system text; never a tool call or an opaque block. In each, the occurrences of `before` from the last to the first: an occurrence's value is the bytes from its end up to the first `after` after it, and it is a candidate when it is 1 to 256 bytes with no line feed. A value never spans two parts.
  - **A numeric slot's candidate** is a number, as above, within the slot's bounds; any other value is skipped.
  - **Where two slots' delimiters overlap, the first declared wins:** a candidate's place is its `before`, its value and its `after`, and within one part, or the system text, a place that shares a byte with a place the scan of an earlier-declared slot takes is not a candidate of the later slot.
  - **Every value once,** at its newest place. The scan stops at 64 candidates. It keeps their places (message, part, offset and length), never copies of them.
- **What it draws,** for each call whose body uses the slot, once per call: with no candidate, the never-issued value; otherwise, with the slot's weight per mille, the never-issued value, else one of the candidates, each as likely. Every use of the slot in that body takes the drawn value. The draws come from the answer's own generator (section 3).
- **Sized before it is copied.** The bound an answer is admitted by before anything is drawn counts each slot's uses at its never-issued value, so an answer whose slots all take that value fits whenever the bound passes; past it, the answer fails as an oversized random answer does now (`ContextTooLong`). The drawn answer is then measured with each slot filled, at its replaced size, against `answer_bytes`, with checked arithmetic, before any of it is copied, as llm.md, 2.6 bounds a take's uses. An answer past it is made again with every slot at its never-issued value, drawing nothing more, so its tools and bodies stay what its key drew.
- **The fake knows only delimiters.** It stays the application's servant: it knows no tool's name, meaning or lifetime. Whether a drawn reference was live, ended and held, consumed, from before the last opening, foreign or never issued is the world's to say, beside the answer the domain gave it, `unknown` where it can't tell. A mention isn't a lifetime: an id can end and still be held until its end is read, and one past the largest the model saw may already be issued.

## 3. Named draws

- **Three seeds.** A world on streams gives the fake three parts of its seed (`../sweeps.md`, 2): the agent's part of `Parties`, `agent/NAME`, NAME the world's name for the model it plays; `Faults`' `llm`; and `Scheduling`'s `llm`. A world may give its one seed instead, and draws as it does now (below).
- **What each draws.**
  - **The agent's,** everything an answer says: whether it calls outside the query's choice, how many calls it makes, each call's tool, whether a call takes an invalid entry and which, each body, each slot's draw, and a text answer's tokens.
  - **The faults',** whatever fails, refuses or cuts an answer: the provider failure roll, the content filter's and the no-call answer's roll (llm.md, 2.3), and a cut's chance and place (llm.md, 2.8).
  - **The scheduling's,** when and where: each reply's, piece's and released hold's latency, and the chance that a request with no scope reads the cache (llm.md, 2.1).
- **A named draw for each request.** An answer's generator is `Rng::new(mix(mix(mix(agent ^ conversation) ^ turn) ^ attempt))`, `mix` as `../sweeps.md`, 2 fixes it, with:
  - **its conversation:** FNV-1a 64, with `../sweeps.md`, 2's basis and prime, over the query's opening, each field written as its length in eight bytes little-endian and then its bytes: the scope's sixteen bytes where the query caches by scope, else nothing; the system text; then each text part's text and each tool output's output of its first message, in order. Within a window the opening stays as it was (`smith/docs/design/domain/session.md`, 1), so a retry, a resumed conversation and every turn of a window share it;
  - **its logical turn:** the number of assistant messages the query holds, as a system cue's script counts its turns (llm.md, 2.4);
  - **its attempt:** the answers this fake has drawn or replayed before for that conversation and turn. A request failed before its answer is drawn (a provider failure, an account's strike, an invalid request, strict's miss, the content filter's or the no-call answer's roll) counts none, so its retry draws what it would have drawn. A request cut after its answer was drawn counts, so the request made again after the cut is the next attempt.
- **The attempts are counted in a bounded table,** `Config.conversations` entries, each a conversation, its turn and the attempts made at it, its room held at configuration under `script_bytes`. A request at another turn of its conversation starts that entry's count again at its turn, and a new conversation, the table full, takes the place of the one asked longest ago. With no entries, every attempt is the first.
- **So, given the three parts, the same request draws the same answer whatever the faults.** A fault that prevents a request or adds a retry changes which requests are made, not what any one of them draws. Two conversations draw apart whatever order their requests arrive in. Two conversations whose openings are the same byte for byte are one conversation to the fake, whose attempts they share.
- **One seed, as before.** A fake given one seed draws as it did: one generator, `Rng::new(seed)`, draws its answers, its faults and its timing, in the order they are drawn now. Its answers still have their keys, which its capture records and a replay answers by, and its attempts are counted. A world that wants its answers drawn by their requests gives the three parts: one on streams takes them from its streams, and one not on streams derives them from its seed through `Streams::new(seed)`, as one on streams does.
- **Scripted turns draw nothing of the agent's.** A script's answer is the application's; the seeded chances before it and the timing after it are drawn as for any answer.

## 4. The capture

- **What it records,** where `Config.capture_answers` is above zero: each answer the agent drew or a capture replayed, in the order made, by its key (conversation, logical turn and attempt), as a `Turn` holds it: its lines, each a call's name and arguments or the answer's text; its finish; and its tokens. Beside each answer, not in it, go its slots' draws, each with its call's position among the answer's lines and its provider id, its slot, its value, whether the value came from the conversation or is the slot's never-issued value, and the world's class of it.
- **The world's class** is a label of 1 to 32 bytes of ASCII letters, digits and `_`, which the world gives a captured slot draw by its call's id and its slot's name, once it knows it (`Domain::classify`). The fake keeps it and never reads it.
- **Bounded:** by `Config.capture_answers` answers and by `Config.capture_bytes`, the owned bytes of its answers, their slots' draws and their classes. The first answer or class that doesn't fit is not kept, nor any answer after it, and the capture says it is full, so a capture is a prefix of what was drawn. With no answers configured, nothing is kept: the worlds count memory.
- **Read** by the world as a copy (`Domain::capture`), when a case ends.
- **Replayed** by a fake configured with it, beside its scripts, under `script_bytes`:
  - **Checked when configured,** and refused as a malformed script is: two answers with one key; an answer with no line, a line other than a call or a text, calls finished otherwise than by tool calls, or a text otherwise than by stop; a slot draw naming a slot the menu doesn't declare or a position past its answer's calls, a value outside 1 to 256 bytes, an empty id, or a class neither empty (not yet given) nor of the label's form.
  - **It answers by key.** A request no script answers whose key the capture holds is answered by that answer, made as the drawn answer was: its calls take ids minted anew, each slot draw taking the new id of the call at its position, and past the query's `max_tokens` it is cut short as llm.md, 2.1 says. It draws nothing of the agent's and counts as an attempt, so a retried or cut request gets the answer it got before, and two conversations keep their own.
  - **A key it lacks** is drawn as without it; under strict (llm.md, 2.5) it fails as a call no script answers does, so a strict replay answers only what it holds.
  - **Faults, timing and every input the fake didn't draw** still come from the seeds, as the fresh-seed campaigns draft replays a case (`temper/docs/design/draft/fuzzy-campaigns.md`, 4.1): a replay under the case's seeds replays its faults too.
- **A finding is pinned as its capture,** a fixed scenario, since a seed draws another agent once a menu or a weight changes.

## 5. Testing

The domain's step tests show, each on its own:
- **slots:** two slots whose delimiters overlap in one part, the first declared taking the place; a value holding `"`, `\` and a control byte, escaped in `{{NAME}}`; an empty candidate set drawing the never-issued value; a numeric slot skipping a value outside its bounds and one that isn't a number; values counted once, newest first, and the scan's bound; an answer one byte over `answer_bytes` with its slots filled made with the never-issued values, and one at it filled; each admission refusal;
- **named draws:** two fakes with identical seeds answering the same requests alike, latencies included; fault isolation, a failure chance or a strike added leaving every answer drawn for a key both runs ask the same; a fake given one seed answering, failing, cutting and timing as it did, random answers included; two conversations' answers independent of their order; a failure before the answer counting no attempt, and a cut counting one;
- **the capture:** a capture replayed with two conversations, in another order, and a cut or a retry, each request getting its own answer; each malformed capture refused; a capture at its bound saying so, a prefix of what was drawn; a class kept beside its slot's draw.

## 6. Why

- **Slots, not a grammar.** The fake already draws random batches. What it lacked was the run's own ids. Slots reuse the take's delimiters, parts and placeholders, so the fake still invents no application name or body.
- **Every value, not the newest.** A take wants the newest value. A slot wants stale ones too, since that is where the faults are. The scan is bounded and keeps places, not copies, within the fake's admission.
- **Sized against `answer_bytes`.** The draft says the fake's admission, `script_bytes`; that bound admits scripts and menus at configuration, and an answer's is `answer_bytes`, which the take's uses are counted against too (llm.md, 2.6). A slot's declaration shares `script_bytes`.
- **Never-issued values when too large,** not another draw: the answer's key fixed its tools and bodies, and the admission before drawing guarantees that the never-issued values fit.
- **The opening as the conversation.** The fake sees no conversation's identity on every dialect: a scope only where the dialect carries a cache key. A window's opening is fixed for its life, and the scope is added where there is one. Turned down: a request's whole text, which a retry after a cut keeps but each turn changes, and an identity the caller passes, which the byte peer would have to read from each dialect's headers.
- **Attempts count answers, not requests.** Counting requests would let a fault before the answer change what the retry draws, and only the answer drawn is the agent's.
- **One seed draws as before.** The draft has every world derive three seeds from its own. Split for every fake given one seed, the faults' and the scheduling's draws would change in every world with a chance or a latency range, smith's, jig's and temper's scripted worlds among them, which no goal of the draft re-picks. Only smith's session world plays random answers in its tests, and it gives the three parts, derived from its seed; every other world keeps its draws. Turned down: answers keyed under one seed with the faults and timing on one generator, which still moves every draw after a random answer, and the three parts derived inside the fake, which would copy the streams' derivation into a crate that knows only `skein-lib`.
- **The class in the capture by call id.** The world sees a call by its provider id, and the fake minted it, so the id joins the world's class to the fake's draw without the world knowing the key.
- **The world classifies, not the fake.** A delimiter marks a mention, not a lifetime or an owner. Only the world knows, so it records each draw's class, `unknown` where it can't tell.
- **Pin the capture, not the seed.** A seed's draws are fixed only while the menu and the weights are. A capture keyed as the draws are replays a retried or cut request with its own answer, and stays a scenario whatever the generator becomes.
