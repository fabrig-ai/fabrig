# whose-red: notes

The reasons behind [whose-red.md](whose-red.md), and what it rests on.

## The inventory, at main c4a6c807ac

Taken on 2026-11-25 by a read-only survey of the change lifecycle and the goal's close against the review of 2026-11-20 (its group on the change lifecycle), and read again where the draft relies on it.

- **Fourteen hold arms,** all present at the review: `Stalled`, `Repairs`, `Resolutions`, `Updates`, `BranchMoved`, `BranchMissing`, `PullClosed`, `Retargeted`, `Failed`, `Flake`, `NoTest`, `Rejected`, `Authority`, `Questions`. A release restores the saved step, clears the stale-write timer and recorded gate failures, zeros repairs, resolutions and updates, resets the stall's origin and fences earlier reads. Where it re-enters still depends on the arm and the saved phase. The engine releases some holds itself, a green read at the head ending a CI hold among them.
- **Reds are judged in five places.** A change's CI shares the landing's job-step classifier, with one runner rerun (`forge.change_policy.reruns`, 1) and the repairs allowance (80). A no-change CI repair reruns and may ask more repairs (F22). A landing gate's runner faults, a missing configured report among them, retry within `gate_retries` (2) and hold (F126), but an empty report still makes a failed receipt (P37). A known flake reruns once per key before a receipt. A landing repair's flake or no-test verdict holds for a release that regates once (F64, F127), and a plain no-change landing repair holds `Failed`, its release asking a fresh repair (P67).
- **The close.** Job kind 4 runs the project's `close_job`, fabrig's `.temper/checked-close`: `.temper/close` settles status, then `pre_pr.py --close` checks the goal's changes, within a 600-second deadline. The worker reads `result.json` after exit 0 or 3. Any other exit, a signal or a missing result fails the job, which jig counts in the run's own class: three more tries, then a hold. The close change holds `Failed` with the job's 128-byte reason and escalates to the goal. A released close checks again only when the goal's tip or its landing branch's tip has moved (ff44375285). pre-PR's cache (b025ef123f) reprints a refusal of budgets, format, citations, outward names or old forms for the same tree, base and counts, but not one with a test failure, and a hit still exits 1 and spends a try.
- **pre-PR's exits.** 1 for a refusal and 1 for an exception caught in its own code. Its `temper/check-results` record, written for deliveries alone, reads `refused` for both. The close's own record, `target/temper/goal-close-pre-pr.json`, is read by nothing in temper.
- **jig's classes** count their tries apart: transient, permanent, run, agent, lost and invalid three each; infrastructure twelve. `Exceeded` holds without a try. A job ends finished, failed or past its deadline, and a class's tries can't be set per job kind.
- **Drift.** A change compares its expected branch head with a fresh read at each step. A lagging pull head waits (F6). A stale update replans or backs off within the stall (F63). A branch hold keeps a pair of heads, one design head observed while another writer was active and one later recorded write, compared by ancestry (F108). A design writer's push is recorded only through a later fresh read, never from its delivery receipt.
- **Goal 13861.** On 2026-11-23 at 23:47Z its run delivered `04b27280`, a merge of the expected `45fdb415`. At 23:51Z the goal held for drift on the kept branch's observation, with no change row to judge it. Released by hand at 00:48Z after the ancestry was checked.
- **The scenarios.** P67's `a_no_change_landing_repair_regates_once`, P37's `a_starved_gate_is_the_runners` and P58's `a_repair_whose_tests_pass_regates_by_itself` are ignored. No scenario covers a refused close against a broken one, or a run's own delivery read as drift.

## Why these choices

- **A red is the change's only when a record names it.** Every misplaced blame in the review (a starved gate repaired as code, a stopped runner counted as red, an unreported gate searched for its culprit) came from treating a missing verdict as a negative one. Requiring the record for a refusal makes the default "not judged", which is retried, not repaired.
- **Three outcomes, not a list of causes.** The site needs to know one thing: whether the change was judged. Passed, refused and broke answer it. The runner's and the check's own differ only in whose allowance they spend and what the hold names.
- **The close refuses by exit 3.** The tooling protocol already has merge's exit 3, a finished job with a result and nothing delivered. A close refusal is the same shape, so no new job end is needed in jig, and the check's own failure stays a failure, retried as any job's.
- **Version 2, not a new exit everywhere at once.** A build reading exit 3 as success with nothing settled would close a red goal. The engine asks for the new exit in the request it writes, so no repository can send it to a build that would misread it.
- **Flakes are the project's statement.** Whether a check can flake depends on what it runs, which only the project knows. A deployment figure can bound reruns, but can't say a project's tests replay. fabrig's do, so its stance is `none` (the user, 2026-11-25), and the rest of temper keeps flake handling for projects whose checks can flake.
- **One check of the same content, then a person.** A repair that changed nothing is evidence the red may not be the change's, but not proof. One automatic check settles a flaky or interrupted red without a person, and a second red at the same content is a fact a person or the goal should see, rather than a loop up to the repairs allowance.
- **One hold, not fourteen.** Each arm's release grew its own exceptions. A cause with words and a single release function keeps the differences in data (what the cause resets) rather than in code paths.
- **Release forgets, then reads.** The bugs fixed one at a time (a stale resolution, a lost delivery, a closed predecessor's pull) all came from a release that kept a remembered fact the fresh read contradicted. Forgetting the held step's memory first makes the fresh read authoritative.
- **A judgement released with nothing changed holds again.** Re-measuring the same inputs gives the same verdict in a project whose checks replay. A person may still ask for it, which covers the projects whose checks can flake.
- **Record writes, not heads.** F6, F63 and F108 each explained one kind of temper's own move at one site. A record of every write, with its precondition, explains them all, and is what makes a forge view behind temper's writes a wait instead of a hold.
- **Bounded record.** A branch written for weeks would otherwise grow without end. Entries are needed only until a read confirms them.
- **The previous build's holds stay held.** Releasing them at the upgrade would act without a person on facts the old build judged. Each converts and waits for its release, which then follows the new rule.
