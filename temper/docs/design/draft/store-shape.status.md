- **Design departs:**
  - 8: skein-kv counts, not jig's component (skein's `data/kv.md`, 2.2): only the store sees each operation it applies with the value it replaces, its snapshots' starts and the frames its recovery replays, and a generic mechanism goes into skein before its consumer. The component gives the rule, names the keyspaces and reports them (jig's `protocol/store/README.md`, 6.3). The designer of S1, 11-13.
  - 8: `temper store census` is `temper ctl DIR deployment census` (`../client/ctl/tools.md`, 2): the `temper` binary's commands are its terminal client, `box` and `help`, a running engine is read by a party through ctl, and ctl's names are a noun of the map, then a part's name (`../client/ctl/tools.md`, 1). The designer of S1, 11-13.
  - 8: the census reaches the deployment's document at most once a minute, not with every commit as the fill does, so that its watchers aren't sent a document per commit (jig's `protocol/store/README.md`, 6.3). The designer of S1, 11-13.
  - 3: the component's own rows, the secrets' store and the transcripts' frames keep their families' versions and patches; only the map's domain families, jig's six and the application's, name their layouts (jig's `protocol/store/layouts.md`, 1): the format row is read before any registry, the secrets' store is copied whole at each upgrade and read once, and the frames are already converted as they are read. The designer of S4, 11-14.
  - 3: a written change converts by its owner's written job at open, over the values at its old layout, not at read (jig's `protocol/store/jobs.md`, 4): step code calls no function an owner hands it, and the application's functions are reached only through the shell; a written change costs a put per value at that layout. The designer of S4, 11-14.
  - 3: a binary's layouts are registered by the open, in the registry's commit, not by the first commit that writes a value at one (jig's `protocol/store/layouts.md`, 5.2): writes then map heads from a table fixed at open, and no commit of the root's grows by a registry row. The designer of S4, 11-14.
  - 3: the frozen-text rule stays for the families a store still patches and for wires (skein's `data/codec/command.md`, 2, step 3), since a landed patch plans against its texts exactly; a registered family keeps no new text, so none of its texts freezes. The designer of S4, 11-14.
  - 3: a renamed declaration, field or variant names its former names in the schema (`was`, skein's `data/codec/README.md`, section 3), which the draft does not say: a plan between a stored layout's text and the binary's must know a rename to tell it from a field dropped and another added, and no patch file carries it any more. The designer of S4, 11-14.
  - 4, step 3: the open makes a new generation when it must write before serving (a patch or a job pending, the registry to seed, a binary layout to register), not whenever the binary differs (jig's `protocol/store/layouts.md`, 5.5): a binary that changes no layout and brings no job writes nothing an older one cannot read, and opens the store in place as today. The designer of S4, 11-14.
  - 3: a value type whose keys do not tell its broad root's variants apart is the root whole, with no variant (skein's `data/codec/README.md`, 9.4; jig's `protocol/store/layouts.md`, 2), not the one alternative its key holds: the tasks' live and ended keys hold a task written before talk (`live`, `ended`) beside one written since (`live_talk`, `ended_talk`), and an ended task is never rewritten, so a layout at one variant would claim values it does not describe; the settings' `ApplicationState` is a root of its own whose first field, `follows`, is an enumeration. A change to any variant of the tasks' `Row` moves the tasks' layout, and a plan converts them at read. The coordinator of S4, 11-14.
  - 3: a key range holds one root (jig's `protocol/store/layouts.md`, 2). The settings' charter versions held two, `CharterVersion` and `OverlaidCharterVersion`, at the same keys, told apart only by decoding by trial, which neither a variant nor a broad root whole can describe. S4 makes them one root, `CharterVersion` with an optional overlay, and adds jig's first written job, `charter-versions`, which puts each stored version again as that root at the first open (jig's `protocol/store/layouts.md`, 6, and jig's `protocol/store/jobs.md`, 4). This upgrade's open therefore writes per charter version stored, a walk of one small range. The coordinator of S4, 11-14.
  - 6: a hold is kept as a row per held phase, not one per hold (jig's `domain/tasks/lifecycle.md`, 12.2): a hold whose `was` moves while it is held, as a run's end heard then moves it, gets a second row, so that the task's `hold`, which escalations read, keeps naming the position of its latest held phase, as it did, and goals' projections keep the identities they have written. A hold's release is its task's history, not a field of its row, so that the row is written once. The designer of S5, 11-14.
  - 3: the tasks family's `Row` keeps its `milestone` variant, and its value type stays declared, holding no value once S5's job has folded the milestones (jig's `domain/tasks/lifecycle.md`, 12.4): the live and ended tasks' value types are the `Row` whole, and a dropped variant would leave their stored layouts with no derived plan, so that dropping it would rewrite every task's record. The designer of S5, 11-14.
  - 6: an ended task's full record outlives its 30 days while live work names it, its requester is a live task, or its person requester has not read it (jig's `protocol/store/retention.md`, 6.2): live work reads its result, a connector recovering a live task's work reads its delegates' records, and the people child's census at start joins each unread result to its record, so erasing any of them would fail a run, a recovery or the start. The deferral counts the horizon again. The designer of S6, 11-14.
  - 6: parties' updates do not each wait 30 days after they are read. A task's updates (its result, its replies, its notices) go with its full record, once read and at least 30 days after it ended, and a live conversation's stay while it lives; a person's decided proposal's update goes 30 days after its decision, read or not (jig's `protocol/store/retention.md`, 6.2 and 6.5). An update keeps no read mark of its own: reads are kept per party's results and per conversation, and a reply's update is found by key only from its activity line. The designer of S6, 11-14.
  - 6: temper's records of a task go with its full record only where temper's own rules let them: its change record, when settled and unpinned (`../domain/forge/change/README.md`, 7). Its landing rows, closers, goals' projections and landed commits keep their own bounds (a table's room, the goal's end), each already bounded. The designer of S6, 11-14.
  - 6: the horizons are engine limits, `retention.turns_ms`, `retention.records_ms` and `retention.summaries_ms`, defaulting to the user's 14, 30 and 365 days (jig's `protocol/store/retention.md`, 2), not members of the deployment's configuration as jig's `protocol/configuration.md`, 3 had them: limits are where a deployment overrides a number by name, as it does the request retention. The designer of S6, 11-14.
