# test-budgets: the reasons behind it

The reasons behind `test-budgets.md`, from the user, 2026-11-16.

- **The ask:** the testing strategy "hasn't been quite honored", and "the only reliable way to enforce it is to put hard guardrails on focused and fuzzy tests budgets": CPU budgets, a very generous wall limit as a last resort for busy builders and a stricter one at the gate, a budget for each layer of the pyramid, and figures that start at what's in place today and converge to the ideal.
- **Their word on the draft's first proposal:** starting at today's figures, raises and cuts alike, "approved"; the gate's wall red after one rerun unless starved, "ok"; over budget red at the gate alone, a warning elsewhere, "ok".
- **A reference for each test,** theirs: "if you add a new test T in layer L, T CPU budget should be within T's reference budget for a single test, or be justified".
- **The ideal,** theirs: "given all temper behavior, functionality, and programming model, what would be the 'theoretical' lower bound limit for the tests?", calibrated by "this number of tests times their weight, respectively, by layer". Its estimate and 100 s: "go ahead".
- **The open items and the hand-off:** "for the open items, I'll go with your recs, just pick what you deem more fit. then send the draft to temper/queue for execution". The picks: the layers' ideals replace temper's worlds' sums, the 8 s being under the estimate's floor; jig's ideal the strategy's 30 s, its floor 26 s; the repository's checks 30 s; the starved reading's quarter as a first figure, the gate reporting each run's stall.
- **The delivery check,** theirs, 11-17, once the milestones had landed: enforcement "both at the gates and in pre-pr, for early catches". The draft's readings: the gate's verdict with main's ledgers, on builders of the CI builder's kind, the ledgers copied there by the gate's runner; a warning where a builder has none; the wall the backstop's.
- **The noise,** 11-17: every change was red where main was over, and the user asked for a fix, "go for 1 and 2", figures unchanged. The draft's readings: a landed figure is a sample, and landing keeps the samples that passed, so main's sits low; the gate's whole runs measure a tree's layers 1 to 4% apart, and two samples differ by more than the 1% deviation a hundredth came from. So main's figure is a median of three, as a test's wall is, and the noise a twentieth, above the spread seen, at least a second for small layers. Left out: a noise from each binary's own spread (three samples give a poor one, and a first ledger none); a cap on an over layer's growth since it went over, since a reference that only falls drifts down with its samples and one fixed at a sample locks the layer once it passes it, as a hundredth did. The cost: a layer over at main may grow by less than the noise at a landing.
- **The lines,** 11-18: the gate's first enforced hour sent nine new tests past their reference back to their goals, and five passed by a reason's line the goal wrote, each approved by an answer citing `tools/tiers/references.md`, 2. The user picked "option 1": every figure in `tools/tiers/budgets.toml` theirs, refused by the gate when a change raises one. Left out: the rule alone, with questions about a line sent to the user (it relies on every agent, and today's followed the design as written); no lines at all, a tree's guide in its testing document the only exception (simplest, but no one-off exception). The user asked that the red send its repair to the strategy, read in full.
- **The catch-up,** 11-18: the user's plan, "put enforcement back asap": land B8 and the docs, list the known outliers that failed gates, watch the gates warn, list what they find the same day, and switch back once no new ones come; squash what is known meanwhile. Only a flagged test can fail a gate on noise: an existing test is held to half again its own figure at main, so the 530 past their reference fail none, and the list is the tests whose figures swing, 14 at first (gate 1415's ledger, gates 1424 and 1427). B8 would have refused the list itself, so a change of `tools/tiers/budgets.toml` alone passes. Left out: a second run alone before a flagged test is red (hides what the strategy's section 10 repairs), held as the fallback if the list doesn't converge.
- **The draft's readings,** not the user's words: the layers as the strategy's tiers, a binary in one; a ledger of main's figures for a partial run; the ratchet's tenth, from the gate's 1% spread; existing tests held to their own figure too, since the growth of 11-12 to 11-15 was existing tests' (`focused-pyramid.md`, 3); a test's CPU measured alone, since a shared process measures none; the guides as references, at today's 90th percentile below the top; moves only down; the wall limits at today's longest run plus a quarter, and three times that elsewhere.
