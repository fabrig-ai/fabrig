# test-budgets: the suites' cost held at the gate, layer by layer

Draft, 2026-11-16, with the user; section 3's delivery check and section 5's noise, 11-17; sections 6 and 8's figures all the user's, the catch-up back to red, and section 6's second measure, 11-18; a gate of the tests that replay, judged by the instructions they retire, 11-20. The testing strategy says what the focused and fuzzy suites may cost (`skein/docs/foundation/testing-strategy.md`, 8 and 8.1), and nothing holds it: the gate reads no budget, and a tree's one figure lets the top of its pyramid take the room of its bottom. This draft puts hard guardrails on both suites: budgets by layer of the pyramid, held at the gate; a reference for each test; a wall limit at the gate that is red; and budgets that start at today's figures and only fall, towards an ideal estimated from a lower bound. The gate runs only the tests that replay, so its verdict is the head's alone, and counts the instructions they retire, which a busy machine doesn't move (the user, 11-20).

## 1. In one page

- **The gate holds the budgets** (section 2). Today it holds none: `tools/tiers/budgets.toml`'s figures for the gate are read by no run, each run's summary says "no budget set", and four trees' suites are over theirs unseen. The CI builder's figures are steady enough to hold: a layer's CPU moves by about 1% from one gate to the next.
- **Instructions are the budget, and the gate is its judge** (section 3; the user, 11-20). The user-space instructions each test process retires, counted by the CPU, summed by layer: a test that does the same work at every run retires the same instructions, busy machine or quiet, where its CPU swings by up to a quarter. CPU is still measured, and reported beside; a run that can't count warns, never red. Over a budget is red at the gate. A delivery check on a builder that keeps main's ledgers holds it too, and refuses the delivery, so a goal hears it before its pull request (the user, 11-17). Any other run (a builder's, anyone's own, a delivery check without the ledgers) prints the same comparison as a warning, since another machine isn't the gate's (the user, 11-16).
- **The gate runs what replays** (section 4; the user, 11-20): step tests and every world below the real loop, in both suites, and the repository's checks. Their verdict is the head's alone, so a main that admits only green heads is green by construction, and a red is the head's. The real loop and end to end, which use the real kernel, run after landing, and a replay of the gate's suites after landing checks that what it runs does replay.
- **A budget for each layer** (section 4). Each tree's focused and fuzzy suites are split into the tiers of the strategy's section 2, with the repository's checks apart; every test binary belongs to one layer, by a table a boundary check keeps whole.
- **A partial run is judged whole** (section 5). The gate runs the packages a change reaches, and takes the others' figures at main from a ledger that every landed gate writes. A layer is red when the change takes it past its budget.
- **Each test has a reference** (section 6): its layer's per-test guide, from the strategy. A new test is measured alone and stays within it; an existing one stays within the larger of it and half again its own figure at main. A test past its reference is red unless the user has given it a line beside the budgets, its figure and its reason; it still counts in its layer. Counting a test run alone is a second pass.
- **Wall time: strict at the gate, generous elsewhere** (section 7). A gate run past its wall limit reruns once, and is red if it passes the limit again without being starved. Everywhere else a backstop three times higher stays a starved warning. With the real kernel out of the gate, its limits follow its new runs.
- **Start at today's figures, then only fall** (section 8). Each budget starts at the gate's figure of 11-16 plus a tenth, raises and cuts alike (the user, 11-16). After each landing a budget falls to its layer's figure at main plus a tenth, where that is lower. Budget moves down the pyramid with the tests a change moves, never up; a raise stays the user's, and the gate refuses one a change makes. The figures turn into instructions once, at the switch, and a layer drifting towards its budget is named, with the landings that grew it, before it is red (the user, 11-20).
- **An ideal beside each budget** (section 9), reported, never enforced. temper's focused suite: 100 CPU s, from a lower bound of 80 to 100 s for its 13,529 tests, against 1,009 s today; its fuzzy suite, the 180 s it has. jig's focused suite: the strategy's 30 s, which its own lower bound of 26 s confirms. The repository's checks: 30 s.

## 2. Where it stands

- **The gate holds no budget.** `tools/tiers/budgets.toml` has a figure for each tree's focused and fuzzy suites, under `[gate.<tree>.<suite>]`. `tools/tiers/cpu.py` checks their shape, and nothing else reads them: each run's summary says "no budget set", and the gate's runner, outside this repository, reads none (`archive/focused-pyramid.md`, 7).
- **The only guard is the wall.** Each profile's `global-timeout` in `.config/nextest.toml`, 300 s focused (a band-aid by the user's word of 11-15, after `default-wall-230.md`'s 230 s) and 600 s fuzzy, which `tools/tiers/one-process` reads (`tools/tiers/design.md`, 11.4). Its expiry is starved, a warning (`skein/docs/foundation/testing-strategy.md`, 8.1), and the gate's runner reruns a starved run once.
- **Today's figures,** the CI builder's gates of 2026-11-16, nine focused runs and eleven fuzzy ones on 8 lanes, against each tree's budget:

| Tree | Focused CPU s (budget) | Fuzzy CPU s (budget) |
|---|---|---|
| temper | 1,009 (1,250) | 472 (180) |
| jig | 161 (215) | 127 (120) |
| smith | 40 (30) | 31 (120) |
| hearth | 26 (30) | 83 (120) |
| skein | 25 (40) | 126 (120) |
| the repository's checks (`fabrig-boundary`) | 66 (none) | none |
| all | 1,329, in 201 to 218 s of wall | 838, in 146 to 171 s |

- **Where it goes:** world tests take two thirds of the focused suite. temper's take 669 of its 1,009 CPU s, its one-process world's suite 416 alone (`archive/focused-pyramid.md`, 2, has the detail).
- **Steady at the gate.** Split by tree and layer, a figure's standard deviation across those runs is about 1%, its largest spread 8%, though changes landed between them; the suites' wall moves by 4%. One test's wall inside a shared process is far noisier: of the tests whose median is 10 to 50 ms, a tenth took at least 2.7 times their median in one of the nine runs.
- **The CI builder** has 8 CPUs. While a gate runs, the builder's other jobs are held to one CPU's time and the gate has the rest; no CPU is reserved for it, and its disk is shared. The steadiness above is what that gives.
- **Busy builders move CPU, not instructions** (11-19 and 11-20). Under the builders' night load a layer of step tests read 19.2 CPU s at main and 21.7 to 24.8 s at changes that didn't touch it. On 11-20, three binaries that replay (a library's step tests, a simulated world and a protocol world), ten runs each on a quiet builder and ten under a gate's load, varied by 1 to 8% in CPU (their coefficient of variation; up to 28% between two runs), and by less than a millionth in the user-space instructions they retired; the four builders that run gates and goals, one CPU model, counted the same binary within ten millionths of each other. A real-loop binary's count varied threefold, since its work follows the kernel's scheduling. Counting cost nothing measurable for the worlds' one thread, and about 23 µs of CPU at each context switch of a counted process, ten times a fixed work's CPU for 32,000 switches: the price of a virtual machine's counter.

## 3. Instructions, judged at the gate

- **The measure is instructions** (the user, 11-20): the user-space instructions each test process retires, its threads and waited descendants included, counted by the CPU's own counter (Linux's `perf_event_open`, opened on the process before it runs its binary and inherited by every task it starts), summed by layer (section 4) for each tree and suite. A test the gate runs does the same work at every run (the strategy's section 1; section 4), so it retires the same instructions, and a budget's verdict is the change's work, never the machine's load (section 2).
- **CPU stays measured:** user plus system time of each test process, its waited descendants included, from `wait4`'s rusage (`tools/tiers/design.md`, 11), reported beside each count. It prices what a count leaves out, the kernel's work and the waits on memory, and is what the ideals were estimated in (section 9), but judges nothing at the gate.
- **Counting needs the kernel's leave.** Debian's `kernel.perf_event_paranoid` of 3 refuses every unprivileged counter; 2, the kernel's own default, lets a user count its own processes' user-space instructions, all a run needs, and the builders that run gates and goals are set to it (11-20). No privileged helper counts for the run: the candidate's tree is what runs, and a helper it could change, run with privilege, would hand it the builder.
- **A run that can't count enforces nothing** (the user, 11-20). Before its suite, a run reads `kernel.perf_event_paranoid` and opens one counter. Past 2, or refused, or with no counter, it says so in one line and why, and its budgets' comparison, in CPU, is a warning, never red: a gate whose builder lost its setting lands what it would have landed without budgets, and its report says enforcement is off.
- **The gate is the judge.** A budget's figures are the CI builder's at the gate, and the gate's verdict on them is red: a change that takes a layer over its budget, or a test past its reference, doesn't land.
- **The delivery check catches it first** (the user, 11-17). On a builder that keeps main's ledgers, the delivery check of `tools/pre-pr/design.md` judges each suite's selection as the gate judges its own: with main's ledger for the suite and the gate's verdict (`tools/tiers/budgets.md`, 5). A layer the delivery takes over its budget, or a new or flagged test past its reference, refuses the delivery with the gate's lines: the layer, the binaries that grew and their tests, or the test and its limit (sections 5 and 6). The goal hears it while its agent still holds the change, not from the gate after its pull request.
  - **Why the gate's verdict holds there.** The builders that run goals are the CI builder's kind: 8 CPUs of the same model, one thread a core (checked 11-17), and they count the same binary within ten millionths of the gate's (11-20), so a delivery's count is the gate's, however busy its builder. Where it judges in CPU, sharing the machine's caches moves its figures, and a delivery refused for a layer the gate then passes is the sign.
  - **Where the ledgers come from.** After each landing the gate's runner copies main's ledger for each suite (section 5) to each builder that runs goals, as `default.json` and `fuzzy.json` in a directory the builder's environment names in `TIERS_LEDGER`, as a deployment names its hold in `TIERS_HOLD` (`temper/docs/design/domain/worker/README.md`). A copy a landing or two old still judges a delivery, and the gate judges again with its own.
  - **Without them,** where the variable is unset, the suite's file is missing or `ledger()` refuses it, the check runs that suite as before, its comparison a warning, and says why in one line: a builder's missing ledger never refuses a delivery.
  - **What it leaves out.** The check builds nothing at its base (`tools/pre-pr/design.md`, 1), so a flagged test is judged by the ledger's figures alone where it has them, and is otherwise a warning (`tools/tiers/references.md`, 6). Main's red stays a warning, as at the gate (section 5). The wall stays the backstop's, a starved warning (section 7). The check's passed-tree cache counts the ledgers it read, so a delivery is judged again when they change.
- **Every other run warns.** A builder's own run, anyone's, `tools/tiers/run`, and a delivery check without the ledgers print the same comparison as a warning naming the layer, its figure and its budget (the user, 11-16). This narrows 8.1's "wherever they run" for the two suites. A count is the same only on the gate's CPU model: code that picks its path by the CPU's features retires other instructions elsewhere.
- **The slow tiers keep theirs.** The browser, process and live tiers keep their budgets in `tools/tiers/budgets.toml` and run as they do: this draft is the focused and fuzzy suites'.

## 4. Layers

- **A layer is a tier of the strategy's section 2:** step tests, domain worlds, system worlds, machine worlds, protocol worlds, io worlds, simulated worlds, the real loop, and end to end against fakes. A tree has a budget for each layer it has, in each suite. The repository's own checks (`fabrig-boundary`) are no layer of a pyramid, and have a budget of their own.
- **Every test binary is in one layer,** since CPU is measured per process and a process runs one binary's tests (`tools/tiers/design.md`, 11). A new table, `tools/tiers/layers.toml`, places them:
  - a library's own tests (a binary id with no `::`, or a `::bin/` one) and a crate's generated codec tests (`skein/docs/foundation/testing-strategy.md`, 2.1) are step tests, by rule, with no line;
  - every other binary has a line: its id and its layer, as its tree's testing document places it (temper's: `temper/docs/design/testing/tiers.md`, 1, where the client's world is a system world, 2.1);
  - a binary whose tests span two layers is split, or takes the higher one until it is.
- **A boundary check keeps the table whole** (`tests/boundary/`): from `cargo metadata`, which builds nothing, every test binary in the workspace has its layer and every line names a binary that exists. The change that adds a binary adds its line, and the change's review reads it against the tree's testing document: what a test checks places it, never its cost (8.1).
- **The gate runs the layers that replay** (the user, 11-20): step tests and domain, system, machine, protocol, io and simulated worlds, in both suites. Each owns its clock, its seeds and its kernel (the strategy's section 1), so a test's verdict and its count are its code's alone. A landing checks the branch merged onto main (`.config/gate/README.md`), so with every landing gated, main is green by construction and a red is the head's: no failure at the base to tell apart, and no base run. The delivery check runs the same layers.
- **The repository's checks stay at the gate, and in the delivery check** (the user, 11-20). Their verdict is the tree's alone, and some guard what landing publishes (`tests/boundary/tests/outward.rs`), which after landing would be too late; a goal hears of them before its pull request, as of the rest. They read the tree and run `cargo metadata`, so their count is checked by the replay below like any binary's.
- **The real loop and end to end run after landing,** at each landing, as the after-landing tier does (`.config/gate/README.md`, After landing). They trade replay for the real kernel, so their failures and their cost can follow its scheduling, which is no head's to answer for at the gate. A red there is the after-landing tier's, repaired from the lowest tier that shows it (the strategy's section 9). Their budgets are section 11's.
- **A binary that mixes them is split first.** About two dozen of the 75 real-loop and end-to-end binaries of 11-20 also hold tests that replay (`skein-kv-tests::suite`'s store functions beside its real ring, for one), and a binary is in one layer, so their replaying tests would leave the gate with them. Before the real loop leaves the gate, each such binary's replaying tests move to a binary of their own, placed by `tools/tiers/layers.toml`.
- **The fuzzy suite's seeds stay fixed at the gate:** the same work at every run (the strategy's section 1). A sweep of fresh seeds finds what fixed ones can't, and its finding is an old fault's, not the head's, so it belongs after landing (section 11).
- **Replay is checked after landing.** Once a day, the after-landing tier runs the gate's suites again at a landed commit and compares each binary's verdict and count with the gate's ledger for that commit. A test whose verdict differs, or a binary whose count moved by more than a hundred-thousandth, doesn't replay: a finding of the after-landing tier, repaired at its cause (the strategy's section 10) or placed in the real loop. The same run gives each test's figures over the days, for a look at tests that grow or swing.

## 5. A partial run, judged whole

- **The gate runs a selection:** the packages a change reaches and those `.config/gate/always.txt` adds (`.config/gate/README.md`, the gate's fourth item). A layer's figure needs all its binaries.
- **The ledger.** Every gate whose candidate lands records what it measured, by the commit it landed: each binary's CPU, and each test's wall and, where the gate ran it alone (section 6), its CPU. A binary's figure at main is the median of its figures in the last three landed gates that ran it whole, as a test's is the median of its last three walls: one run's figure is a sample, and a landing keeps the samples that passed, so the last alone sits low. A count is no sample: a binary's count at main is its last landed count, each landing's kept beside it, so the ledger says what each landing added to each binary. The ledger is the gate's own record, kept beside its reports outside this repository, as its record of the base's failures is.
- **A layer's figure at the gate** is what the gate measured for the layer's binaries in its selection, plus the ledger's figures for the others. The same sum at main is the layer's figure there.
- **The red is the change's.** A layer over its budget at the head and within it at main is red. Over at both, the change is red only where it adds more than the noise, a twentieth of the layer's figure at main and at least a second: otherwise the red is main's, as a failure the base has too is. The gate's whole runs measure the same tree's layers up to 4% apart (11-17), so a hundredth, from section 2's 1% deviation, held nearly every change red where main was over. A layer over at main may so grow by less than the noise at a landing; its line names it at every run, and its repair is 8.1's.
- **Counted, there is no noise to allow for** (the user, 11-20). Over at main, a change is red where it adds more than a hundredth of the layer's count there: a policy, not a noise, so a change may still add a test while the layer's repair is pending, and the drift of section 8 names the sum of such landings. A run that can't count compares in CPU, as a warning, with the twentieth and the second (section 3).
- **A red names its cause:** the layer, its figure and its budget, and the binaries that grew against main, largest first, each with its tests that grew by wall. The goal is told where to look.
- **A binary the ledger lacks** counts by what the gate measures, and its tests are new (section 6). An empty ledger is filled by a whole run of the gate's own before any budget is held.

## 6. Each test's reference

- **The references are the strategy's per-test guides** (`skein/docs/foundation/testing-strategy.md`, 8), placed by layer in `tools/tiers/budgets.toml`, a tree's testing document setting its own where it has one:

| Layer | A focused test | A fuzzy test |
|---|---|---|
| step tests | 40 ms | 10 s |
| domain, machine, protocol and io worlds | 0.2 s | 10 s |
| system worlds | 0.6 s | 10 s |
| simulated worlds, the real loop, end to end | 1 s; temper's 2 s (`temper/docs/design/testing/checks.md`, 1) | 10 s |
| the repository's checks | 1 s | none |

  temper's tests at their 90th percentile today sit at these figures below the top of the pyramid (step tests 45 ms, domain worlds 210 ms, system worlds 299 ms), and well past them at the top (the one-process world's median test 3.6 s, the real loop's 2.3 s).
- **A new test,** one the ledger lacks, is run alone after the suite, in a process of its own: its CPU is that process's, less the same binary's run selecting no test. It stays within its layer's reference, or is red.
- **An existing test** stays within the larger of its layer's reference and half again its own CPU at main: one already past the reference keeps its place, and doesn't grow. Its wall in the shared run flags it, when past twice its figure at main and at least 50 ms over it, above the shared run's noise (section 2). A flagged test is run alone at the head and at the base, as the gate already reruns a failure at the base, and judged by the two CPU figures.
- **Measured again before red** (the user, 11-18). One run alone is a sample, and below a tenth of a second a test's CPU alone swings with its process past half again on the same code: B9's repair in jig found no cause in tests whose figures did (ordered maps, a fixed seed, no entropy, clock or thread), and most of the tests the catch-up listed are that small. So a test run alone after the suite whose figure passes its limit, with main's ledger figure standing for the base's until the base has run, runs alone twice more, and its figure at the head is the lowest of its three; at the base, a flagged test runs alone three times, and its figure there is the highest. A test is red only where its lowest at the head passes the limit its highest at the base sets, or its reference where it is new. A test whose three figures at the head lie past half again apart is named as swinging, beside B9's warning.
- **Counted, in a second pass** (the user, 11-20: "leave tests ran alone to a second pass, let's go by steps"). The first pass counts the layers alone; a test run alone stays measured in CPU, as above, and a test past its reference stays a warning at the gate, as the user left it on 11-19. In the second pass, a test run alone takes its count less its binary's empty run's: exact, so it runs once at the head, and a flagged test is judged by its count against its count at main, from the ledger; the three runs above stay CPU's alone. The references and the user's lines turn into instructions then.
- **Past it, by the user's line.** A test may pass its reference by a line in `tools/tiers/budgets.toml`: its binary and name, the CPU it may take, and why, in a sentence. The line is the user's figure, as a budget is (the user, 11-18): a change never adds or raises one (section 8). A test past its reference is placed in the tier it belongs to, or made cheaper, the strategy's 8.1; failing both, its measurements go to the user, who writes its line. Such a test still counts in its layer, so a reason never raises a budget, and past its own line's figure it is red again.
- **The same-work backlog** (the user, 11-18). A test whose CPU alone swings past half again on the same code breaks the strategy's section 10, and a gate can flag it on noise: run alone at a low base and a high head, it passes its limit with no change to it. Until its repair, it takes a line of half again its largest figure, rounded up, which the repair drops. The list of them is the user's, in `tools/tiers/budgets.toml`, and grows as the gates find more (`test-budgets.execution.md`, 2).
- **What a reference catches that a layer can't:** an expensive test on the day it is written, by name, while its layer still has room; and one test that doubles inside its layer's tenth of headroom.

## 7. Wall time

- **At the gate, red.** Each suite has a wall limit at the gate, 270 s focused and 215 s fuzzy: the longest of section 2's runs, plus a quarter (the user, 11-16). The gate passes it to its run through a configuration of its own (`tools/tiers/design.md`, 11.2). A run past it is stopped and rerun once, as now. Past it again, it is red, unless the gate was starved: its own cgroup recorded full CPU or io stalls (the kernel's pressure accounting) for at least a quarter of the run's wall. The quarter is a first figure: the gate's report gives each run's stall, and the figure follows them. A starved run stays the warning it is today, its tests unobserved.
  - The limit catches what CPU can't see: a test that sleeps or waits on the real clock, tests a test group holds to one at a time, and a process so long that the other lanes wait on it.
  - It narrows 8.1, where a wall limit's expiry is always starved, at the gate only. A harness's own deadline keeps 2.10's rule.
- **Elsewhere, generous.** Each profile's `global-timeout`, which every other run reads, becomes three times the gate's limit, 810 s focused and 645 s fuzzy, a last resort for builders that may be busy (the user, 11-16). Its expiry stays starved, a warning. It replaces the band-aid of `default-wall-230.md`, which goes when it lands.
- **The wall limits aren't ratcheted.** A selection's wall says nothing of the whole suite's: the limits move with the ideals, by the user's word.
- **Without the real kernel** (section 4), a gate's run waits on no real io: a test hung on one can no longer keep the gate's cgroup stalled and pass as starved. Its limits are the user's figures for the new runs, their longest plus a quarter as on 11-16, set when the real loop and end to end leave it; until then they stay.

## 8. Starting, then only falling

- **The starting figures** are the gate's of 2026-11-16 (section 2's runs, their median) by layer, plus a tenth and at least a second, raises and cuts alike (the user, 11-16). For the trees' totals that comes to about:

| Tree | Focused: today's budget, its start | Fuzzy |
|---|---|---|
| temper | 1,250, 1,110 | 180, 520 |
| jig | 215, 180 | 120, 140 |
| smith | 30, 45 | 120, 35 |
| hearth | 30, 29 | 120, 92 |
| skein | 40, 28 | 120, 139 |
| the repository's checks | none, 73 | none |

  Each layer's figure is written by the milestone that writes the layers' budgets, from the same runs, under `tools/tiers/layers.toml`.
- **The ratchet.** After each landing, a layer's budget falls to its figure at main (section 5) plus a tenth, at least a second, where that is lower than the budget. What a repair wins is kept, and the next change can't spend it; nothing raises a budget by itself. The figure in `tools/tiers/budgets.toml` is the user's ceiling. The budget the gate holds is the lower of that ceiling and the layer's lowest figure at main since the ceiling was last set, plus the tenth; the gate's report prints both.
- **Budget moves down the pyramid.** A change that places tests lower (the strategy's section 9; `archive/focused-pyramid.md`, 4) moves their room with them: a line in `tools/tiers/budgets.toml` moves CPU seconds from one layer's budget to that of a layer below it in the strategy's table, in any tree. The gate checks only that each move goes down and leaves the sum as it was, and applies it to the ceiling and the held budget alike. A move up is a raise, and a raise is the user's (8.1).
- **Every figure is the user's** (11-18). The gate refuses a change that raises a figure of `tools/tiers/budgets.toml` or adds a reason's line (section 6), checked against its base as the citations are: a layer's ceiling, a reference, a line, a suite's limit. Lowering one, dropping a line and a move down pass. A change that touches `tools/tiers/budgets.toml` and nothing else passes whatever it does there: it is how the user's figures land, a drafter's mechanical change on their word, while every goal's landing touches more (the user, 11-18). On 11-18, five of the nine new tests past their reference passed by a line their own goal wrote, each approved by an answer that cited the design's "the change writes its line".
- **The figures turn into instructions once** (the user, 11-20), when the gate's verdict turns to counts: each layer's ceiling and ideal, and in the second pass its references and the user's lines (section 6), times its layer's instructions per CPU second at main, the median over the gates that measured both before the switch. The rate differs by layer, about 6 to 11 billion a second on 11-20, so each layer converts at its own, and the report gives each figure in instructions and, at that fixed rate, in CPU seconds. The converted figures are the user's, and land as theirs do, by a change of `tools/tiers/budgets.toml` alone. The ratchet's floor of a second becomes ten billion instructions. A move down from a layer judged in CPU (section 11) converts at the receiving layer's rate.
- **Drift is named before it is red** (the user, 11-20). The held budget leaves a layer a tenth above its lowest figure at main. Once its figure at main is eight hundredths above that lowest, the gate's report names the layer at every run, a warning, with the landings that grew it most since its lowest and their binaries, which the ledger's counts give exactly, with no run: what got worse, by commit. Looking into it is the after-landing tier's, started by hand on the user's yes, as its repairs are; a look that starts by itself is "just not yet" (the user, 11-20).
- **The red sends its repair to the strategy.** Each budget red, reference red and refusal, at the gate and in the delivery check, ends with one line: read `skein/docs/foundation/testing-strategy.md` in full before repairing it, then 8.1's three questions in order. A goal's repair reads the gate's log, so the line is where it looks.

## 9. The ideal

- **Beside each budget, its ideal,** the user's figure, in `tools/tiers/budgets.toml`. The gate's report gives each layer's figure, budget and ideal; nothing is red for missing an ideal. Repairs bring the figures down (`archive/focused-pyramid.md`, for one), the ratchet keeps what they win, and the gap to the ideal is what's left to do.
- **Without an estimate, the strategy's figures,** for a tree's totals only: 30 s focused and 120 s fuzzy, skein's focused 40 s, temper's fuzzy 180 s (`tools/tiers/budgets.toml`'s notes). A tree's split by layer comes with its estimate, as temper's and jig's below. skein and hearth meet theirs already.
- **temper's focused suite: 100 CPU s,** from a lower bound. The question, the user's: given all of temper's behaviour, its functions and its programming model, what must its tests cost at least? It has no exact answer. The estimate is, for each layer, its count of tests times what one test must cost at least, from the gate's focused run of 2026-11-16 12:04Z:

| Layer | Tests | CPU s today | Its median test | Count × median, s | Least a test costs | Count × least, s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
