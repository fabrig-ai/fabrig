# whose-red: notes

The reasons behind [whose-red.md](whose-red.md), and what it rests on.

## The inventory, at main c4a6c807ac

Taken on 2026-11-25 by a read-only survey of the change lifecycle and the goal's close against the review of 2026-11-20 (its group on the change lifecycle), and read again where the draft relies on it.

- **Fourteen hold arms,** all present at the review: `Stalled`, `Repairs`, `Resolutions`, `Updates`, `BranchMoved`, `BranchMissing`, `PullClosed`, `Retargeted`, `Failed`, `Flake`, `NoTest`, `Rejected`, `Authority`, `Questions`. A release restores the saved step, clears the stale-write timer and recorded gate failures, zeros repairs, resolutions and updates, resets the stall's origin and fences earlier reads. Where it re-enters still depends on the arm and the saved phase. The engine releases some holds itself, a green read at the head ending a CI hold among them.
- **Reds are judged in five places.** A change's CI shares the landing's job-step classifier, with one runner rerun (`forge.change_policy.reruns`, 1) and the repairs allowance (80). A no-change CI repair reruns and may ask more repairs (F22). A landing gate's runner faults, a missing configured report among them, retry within `gate_retries` (2) and hold (F126), but an empty report still makes a failed receipt (P37). A known flake reruns once per key before a receipt. A landing repair's flake or no-test verdict holds for a release that regates once (F64, F127), and a plain no-change landing repair holds `Failed`, its release asking a fresh repair (P67).
- **The close.** Job kind 4 runs the project's `close_job`, fabrig's `.temper/checked-close`: `.temper/close` settles status, then `pre_pr.py --close` checks the goal's changes, within a 600-second deadline. The worker reads `result.json` after exit 0 or 3. Any other exit, a signal or a missing result fails the job, which jig counts in the run's own class: three more tries, then a hold. The close change holds `Failed` with the job's 128-byte reason and escalates to the goal. A released close checks again only when the goal's tip or its landing branch's tip has moved (ff44375285). pre-PR's cache (b025ef123f) reprints a refusal of budgets, format, citations, outward names or old forms for the same tree, base and counts, but not one with a test failure, and a hit still exits 1 and spends a try.
- **pre-PR's exits.** 1 for a refusal and 1 for an exception caught in its own code. Its `temper/check-results` record, written for deliveries alone, reads `refused` for both. The close's own record, `target/temper/goal-close-pre-pr.json`, is read by nothing in temper.
- **pre-PR's per-test rerun** runs only in its goal mode (`.temper/goal-pre-pr`), which the full goal pre-PR workflow runs on a CI runner. Close mode reports a failure as it ran. The runner's fresh checkouts have no `temper/rerun`, so the bound is 0. Read on the reference deployment on 2026-11-25: 29 retained checker logs of 11-22 had 26 `pre-pr: tests again: none (…, at most 0 run again)` lines, and no test was run again or passed as a flake. The worker-written figure was set to 0 there that day all the same, as a guard.
- **jig's classes** count their tries apart: transient, permanent, run, agent, lost and invalid three each; infrastructure twelve. `Exceeded` holds without a try. A job ends finished, failed or past its deadline, and a class's tries can't be set per job kind.
- **Drift.** A change compares its expected branch head with a fresh read at each step. A lagging pull head waits (F6). A stale update replans or backs off within the stall (F63). A branch hold keeps a pair of heads, one design head observed while another writer was active and one later recorded write, compared by ancestry (F108). A design writer's push is recorded only through a later fresh read, never from its delivery receipt.
- **Goal 13861.** On 2026-11-23 at 23:47Z its run delivered `04b27280`, a merge of the expected `45fdb415`. At 23:51Z the goal held for drift on the kept branch's observation, with no change row to judge it. Released by hand at 00:48Z after the ancestry was checked.
- **The scenarios.** P67's `a_no_change_landing_repair_regates_once`, P37's `a_starved_gate_is_the_runners` and P58's `a_repair_whose_tests_pass_regates_by_itself` are ignored. No scenario covers a refused close against a broken one, or a run's own delivery read as drift.

## Why these choices

- **A promise of a record binds; its absence elsewhere doesn't.** Every misplaced blame in the review (a starved gate repaired as code, a stopped runner counted as red, an unreported gate searched for its culprit) came from reading a missing verdict as a negative one. But most of today's code reds show only as a failed status or a failed step. So a check that promises a record and doesn't deliver one didn't judge, while a site that never promised one keeps today's rule.
- **Candidate diagnostics against the machine's errors.** A compiler error in a helper the check builds from the candidate is the change's, though it's not a test. A signal, a full disk or an exception in the checker's own code isn't. The line runs between what the candidate's tree caused and what the check or its machine did.
- **A shown red stands.** A runner's fault in one job says nothing about a red another job showed, so the red wins.
- **Three outcomes, not a list of causes.** The site needs to know one thing: whether the change was judged. Passed, refused and not judged answer it. The runner's and broke differ only in whose allowance they spend and what the hold names.
- **The close's exit 3 gains a type.** The worker already reads a result after exit 3, as merge's finished job with nothing delivered. A close refusal is the same shape, so no new job end is needed in jig, and the check's own failure stays a failure, retried as any job's.
- **Asked for at both ends, by the environment.** A build reading a refused close as a finished one with nothing settled would close a red goal, and fabrig's close command refuses any request but version 1. An environment variable set by a worker that says it can carry the refusal reaches a new command and is ignored by an old one. The engine asks only such a worker, and checks the result against the attempt.
- **Flakes are the project's statement.** Whether a check can flake depends on what it runs, which only the project knows. A deployment figure can bound reruns, but can't say a project's tests replay. fabrig's do, so its stance is `none` (the user, 2026-11-25), and the rest of temper keeps flake handling for projects whose checks can flake.
- **One check of the same content, then the goal or a person.** A repair that changed nothing is evidence the red may not be the change's, but not proof, and two reds at the same content may as well confirm a regression the repair failed to fix. One automatic check settles a flaky or interrupted red without anyone. A second red at the same content is a fact for the goal or a person to judge, rather than a loop up to the repairs allowance, so the hold says the red stands and blames no one.
- **The measurement has an identity.** A landing makes a new key at the same head, and an old receipt can still be read. Storing which check was repaired and which measurement replaces it keeps an old answer, or a restart, from passing for the fresh one.
- **One hold, not fourteen.** Each arm's release grew its own exceptions. A cause with words and a single release function keeps the differences in data (what the cause resets) rather than in code paths.
- **Release settles, then forgets, then reads.** The bugs fixed one at a time (a stale resolution, a lost delivery, a closed predecessor's pull) all came from a release that kept a remembered observation the fresh read contradicted. But some facts can't be read again from the forge: a producer that finished with no change looks the same as one that never ran, and a landing may still owe its result. Those are settled first and kept. Only reads, pins and verdicts the fresh read replaces are forgotten.
- **A judgement released with nothing changed holds again.** Re-measuring the same inputs gives the same verdict in a project whose checks replay. A fresh measurement is its own act, revisioned, one per request, and taken by whoever decides the hold, which covers the projects whose checks can flake. It isn't `--again`, which jig keeps for an uncertain effect.
- **Record writes, not heads.** F6, F63 and F108 each explained one kind of temper's own move at one site. A record of every write explains them all, and is what makes a forge view behind temper's writes a wait instead of a hold.
- **Before and after, not a commit.** A matching commit doesn't say who moved the ref: another actor can reset a branch to an older commit of temper's, or close a pull without moving its head. Each entry keeps its operation's before and after state and its order, and where the forge can't tell, the caller reads again within its bound, then holds saying so.
- **Admitted before the forge sees it.** A worker pushes to git itself, so a record written from the run's answer would come after the forge already shows the commit. The worker waits for the engine's admission instead, at the cost of one exchange per push.
- **Room waits, and nothing unsettled is evicted.** An uncertain write evicted would turn into drift at the next read. So a full record makes the next write wait, and only confirmed entries go.
- **The previous build's holds stay held, and its open comparisons stay open.** Releasing them at the upgrade would act without a person on facts the old build judged. Each converts and waits for its release, which then follows the new rule. A pair of heads still to compare, or a writer whose push may have reached the forge, would become confirmed history if taken as settled, so each converts as an entry still owed its comparison or readback.
