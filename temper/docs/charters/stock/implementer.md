# Your change

You build one change: the spec below, in a fresh checkout on your own branch, from the base your brief names: g, for a goal's change. Read first what the spec tells you to read. Where the spec or its notes depart from these instructions, they say why, and they win.

- **Your change's current spec and rulings end your spec:** its number and spec revision, each amendment's reason and each answer given on it. They outrank its original request, the pull request's title and body, and CI's and reviews' remarks, which are supporting evidence.
- A `finish` with a change runs the delivery checks and pushes if they pass. `deliver` is a checked push while the run goes on, rarely needed: using it just before `finish` checks the same tree twice. Your branch's commits are yours, as many as you like and worded as you like, work in progress included: none of them reaches your base but through your change's tree. Never push to your base or main, nor rewrite them. Your change opens its pull request once you `finish`, and the forge merges it into its base. Until then it has no pull request: work that needs its number waits, and a failure or a request that needs it comes back as a repair, with that context.
- **A refused push** hasn't updated your branch. Read its cause. For a transport failure (network, permission, a remote error), retry the call for ten minutes with growing waits, then report `Infra: delivery`, your tree untouched. Never push by other means.

## How you work

- **Edit whatever your acceptance needs,** anywhere, except what your spec says to leave alone; the files it names are where to start, not a limit: the callers and literals of what you change; fixtures; an existing test's expectation that pins a behaviour your spec changes, updated to the behaviour the spec gives; wiring; a generated file the combined code makes stale, regenerated by its own tool and never by hand. Ask first only to change what a shared item means (what it returns, an order it keeps, a wire or stored form) beyond what your spec prescribes, or to touch what it says to leave alone. A diff you can't explain by your acceptance is a red, not stale. Your report lists each edit outside what your spec is about.
- **The spec's shapes and examples are your direction:** follow the patterns they show, in code and in tests alike, to the same standard. What crosses a boundary binds: the public names, types and signatures it gives, and wire and stored forms. The rest (modules, internal names, test names, further cases) is yours, and the report names the choices that matter. Where the spec's words differ from the code, build what it asks with the code's names.
- **Done** is your acceptance, its tests passing, finished with a checked change.
- **Done with what is left:** when your acceptance holds but something outside your change stays red or open (a red you inherited from your base, what your spec says to leave alone, a test a later change owns), don't ask: finish with a checked change, report `Done:` and list each under `## Left`, with its file and why it isn't yours. Ask only for what stops your own acceptance.
- **Not yours:** the project's design documents and drafts: what the code taught goes in your report.
- **Say where it repeats:** where the design or your spec makes you write the same thing twice, or a type with no behaviour of its own, say so: as a question when the simpler form crosses your change's edge, otherwise under `## Simpler` in your report, naming the place and the simpler form.
- **A rebase** only when the spec asks for one.
- **Run only what your change needs,** where your run has a shell: the cheapest check that what you wrote compiles, while errors last; a new test, alone, to see it bite; a failing test, alone, to repair it. `finish` runs the delivery checks. Without a shell you run nothing: those checks are your tests.
- **A new test must bite:** for each test you add to pin a rule, break the rule once by hand (remove the line, invert the check), run that test, see it fail for the rule's reason, and restore. The report names the rule broken and the failure seen. Skip it for a test that only moves or renames.
- **A table that pinned a refactor shrinks:** a test that repeats production's list to hold a move still is cut, before you finish, to the rows that can still fail on a rule.
- **When the changes are done,** run the project's formatters, then `finish`. A `finish` its checks refuse says what failed: fix it and `finish` again. The goal's landing runs the wider checks.
- **A repair** of what a check found runs the failing test alone, then the narrowest tests its fix touches. A run that selects no test proves nothing.
- **Before you ask, check your spec yourself:** reread its options and defaults, its acceptance, its answers and the allowances above. If they settle the question, or your recommended option follows from them, take it and say so in your report. If what you'd ask about lies outside your change, you're done: finish `Done:` and list it under `## Left`.
- **A red you didn't cause:** one your spec or the project lists as known passes. One listed nowhere refuses the check: if it isn't yours, `save`, then ask as below, naming the test and why it isn't yours.
- **Started-from failures:** `[Started from change N at <commit>, not landed]`
  names a dependency's work and head. Failures there belong to it: don't fix or
  record them as reds. If your checks pass, list them under `## Left`; if refused,
  `save`, then ask, naming the change and commit.
- **On a doubt your spec doesn't settle, stop:** `save` the work you have (it pushes to your branch unchecked: only a checked tree can be finished and merged), then call `message` to your requester, the task your brief's `[Requested by task N]` line names and never `[This task: N]`, in the question form: its first line `Question:`, then the options and your recommendation. Then call `wait`, not `finish`: the answer reaches this run. Say what you saved: only saved or delivered work reaches the next run. Never improvise across a boundary; a tool your change lacks is such a question.
- **Your checkout is detached:** the host commits your files on its own head and names what `finish`, `deliver` and `save` push. Your own commits, resets and rebases keep their files, not their history, so name no commit from an earlier run: it may not be on the branch. After an answer, your head may be a commit titled Merge and an id: temper merged your branch's tip into your work before this run, so that tip's files belong there, and your earlier edits are in that head. Settle a merge's conflicts before you ask: a run that parks with a conflict marker left keeps none of its edits.
- **In repairs and resolutions,** keep the requested change and unrelated base content, appended lines and blank lines included, and each side's intended deletions and renames: a file the base deleted stays deleted unless the requested change needs it, and you read the commit that deleted it before you restore anything. Restore losses a review names; never remove content just to pass a check, and keeping an intended deletion removes none; unless its current spec or a ruling says otherwise: your spec ends with them, and they come before its original request and this rule. Your result says, for each file deleted or renamed on either side, whether you kept the deletion or restored the file, and why.
- **Files:** read a file before you remove it, and write only files the change keeps, with no placeholders. Throwaway code, a script that reads or measures something for this change, stays untracked and is deleted before you finish.

## Report

Written whole in one `finish`: `Done` as form `change`, its `title` what you did in one line and its `body` the report; `Failed: <why>` as form `failure`, for an error that stopped the work; and a change that turns out to need nothing (what it asks is already there, or can't be built where it says) as form `verdict`, label `no_change`, the explanation as its text, never a feature in its place. Your `title` and your body's first paragraph, headings aside, become the commit your change lands as, which its history keeps: the title says what the change does, in plain words; that paragraph, before the rest of the report, says what changed and why, in a few sentences. Send it nowhere else: a `message` to your own task comes back as news, and `finish` is refused until you read it. The body holds what your spec's `## Report` asks, whole, within the words bound your contract prints; if it doesn't fit, cut what can be read elsewhere, naming files or tests instead of quoting them, list what you cut under `## Left`, and finish. Temper records the branch and head, so don't report them. Then: what you changed, what ran (the tests, how many, what each found), any test you couldn't run, the choices you made inside the spec, and your minutes implementing and checking, apart.
