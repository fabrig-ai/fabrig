# temper's command lines

How temper's binaries meet their command lines, on the repository's
convention (`skein/docs/foundation/cli.md`) and skein's table
(`skein/docs/design/runtime/shell.md`, 14): each binary's package and
crate, its table, how its arguments reach a command, its help, its
exits, the environment it reads and passes down, and the reference
documents generated from its table. The sets are the draft's
(`../draft/cli.md`, section 4); this document gives `temper-box`'s,
`temper-engine`'s, `temper-worker`'s and `temper-agent`'s. `temper`, the
client, takes its in its own milestone (`../draft/cli.execution.md`,
C3).

## 1. In one page

- **A binary is a package** at `temper/bin/<binary>/`, whose `main.rs`
  installs fail-stop and calls its crate's `main` (2). The box's crate
  is `temper-box-shell`, the engine's `temper-engine-shell`, the
  worker's `temper-worker-shell` and the agent's `temper-agent-shell`.
- **Its crate holds its table,** a `skein_shell::Table`, and captures
  the process's arguments and environment once (3). Every other reader
  takes what the capture holds.
- **`Table::reach`** answers what the arguments reach
  (`skein/docs/design/runtime/shell.md`, 14.4): the page, the version,
  a command's page, a command, or no command (3).
- **Exits:** 0 done, 1 failed, 2 refused its arguments (4).
- **Help:** `-h`, `--help` and `help` print the table's page;
  `help pages`, `help NAME`, `help --search` and `help commands` are the
  pages, and `commands` is the binary's own table (5).
- **The tables** of `temper-box` (6) and of the deployment's three,
  `temper-engine`, `temper-worker` and `temper-agent` (7), and the
  variables each declares (8).
- **Generated:** each binary's `<binary>-args.md` and `<binary>-env.md`,
  and the tree's common environment document, beside this page, checked
  byte for byte (9).

# temper's client

Provisional, 2026-10-14, revised 2026-11-07. temper's client: temper's own
presentation on jig's client, `jig-client`. Its crates; its client root,
which holds `jig-client` and temper's pages, the LLM accounts' and the
forges' among them; the forge's client family, its words, its one form
and its requests; its view, on
skein's UI kit; the terminal that `temper-box open` runs, on skein's text
shell; signing in and the small store; what temper's server does for it;
and how it is tested. jig's client is jig's `client/domain/client/README.md`, its
API jig's `client/domain/contract/README.md` and its protocol jig's
`protocol/client/README.md`: this document takes them as given and cites them.
The forge's parts of the client are `forge/README.md`, the browser `web/README.md`,
and the agents' tools, `temper ctl`, `ctl/README.md`. temper is the source
of truth for what it draws; the mockups (`web/mockups/`) design what it
does not draw yet: their look, their parts and their order, their
words and where each act leads. This document holds what each page
reads, watches and does, and cites them for the rest
(`web/mockups/README.md`, section 1). What is still open is listed in
section 5.

## 1. In one page

- **temper's presentation, on jig's client.** temper's client root
  holds `jig-client` as a component (jig's `client/domain/root.md`):
  the link, watches by handle, keyed requests until durable, the work
  kept across a restart, and the forge's family carried as bytes. Its
  pages, words, confirmations and navigation are temper's own. jig's
  reference UI (jig's `client/domain/pages.md` and jig's
  `client/domain/view.md`) is not temper's: temper arranges no page of
  jig's, and copies no client root, view root, native client or card of
  jig's.
- **Its pages,** on jig's (jig's `client/domain/pages.md`): the inbox
  (home), the chats, a chat, a project's goals, a task's page, the
  changes, the forge's adoption form, the forges, and the LLM accounts
  (`pages/README.md`); "How work runs" and a proposal of settings' review (`pages/settings.md`, 2).
  The mockups design them: their look, parts, words and acts. What no page
  shows is open (section 5).
- **Its cards are its own.** A proposal, a question, a held task, a
  person task, a result, an entry of activity, an outcome and each
  confirmation are temper's nodes, drawn from the object's document as
  `jig-client` delivers it and from `jig-client`'s `facts` (jig's
  `client/domain/client/helpers.md`), as jig's
  `client/domain/objects.md`, section 2 allows an application. temper
  calls no renderer of jig's reference UI, and copies none of its
  modules, functions or phrase tables.
- **On jig's API alone.** What a page shows, it watches through jig's
  subjects (`me`, `inbox`, `tasks{query}`, `task`, `tree`, `run`,
  `accounts`) or
  reads (`activity`, `proposal`; jig's `client/domain/contract/README.md`,
  sections 4 and 5); what a person does is jig's operations (jig's
  `client/domain/contract/requests.md`), through jig's helpers where
  they fit (3.6).
- **The confirmation is temper's.** An act that cannot be undone says
  what it will do before it is sent, from the facts jig's documents
  give, and every deciding act names the revision the person was shown
  (3.6).
- **LLM accounts, made from the client.** On the accounts pages,
  `Accounts` and its flow `ConnectModel`, a person signs in with an LLM
  provider, declares a program's login, or adds a key, pasted once and
  never kept (`pages/README.md`, `README.md`, 3.6); while no account their work may use is usable,
  a banner on every page says so and offers the three (`pages/accounts.md`, 2). A chat's
  composer and a task's page name the account and model the work runs
  on. A browser opens a sign-in's address in a new tab while the page
  waits; the terminal prints the address and opens no browser (`line.md`, 5).
  The accounts are jig's, kept by its engine
  (`../protocol/credentials.md`, section 3).
- **A first run's playground.** When the person chooses it, the
  engine makes the playground, a fake forge, the scripted model's
  account and the playground's project (`../protocol/setup/playground.md`),
  and the client reads it from the engine (`forge/family.md`, 7): a banner of one line
  on every page says it is the playground and offers "set up temper",
  which resets it and opens the welcome's Forge step, and the
  playground's `NewChat` suggests the requests its model
  plays (`pages/welcome.md`, 2). The printed link's sign-in in a browser lands on the
  welcome where the document shows it, and on the inbox otherwise
  (`web/README.md`, 2.4).
- **The forge's family.** Its words for the connector, a repository, a
  branch, a pull request and each kind of write need no document (`forge/family.md`, 1).
  Its other parts are documents its client side reads, with requests of
  their own (`forge/README.md`); a part it does not read says "Shown on the
  forge." (`forge/family.md`, 2). Its form is the adoption, with a picker of the
  repositories temper can reach on the chosen forge and "Not listed"
  for the others, sent through `jig-client`'s request table as the
  forge's bytes, like any other keyed request (`forge/family.md`, 3 to 5 and `forge/records.md` and `forge/family.md`, 6).
- **Forges connected from the client.** On the forges' pages, `Forges`
  and its flow `ConnectForge`, a person whose role holds the
  deployment's connectors (in standalone, the owner) sets up or
  connects a forge, which the set up or the connect makes as a record
  the store keeps (`../protocol/forge/engine.md`, 1 and 2), installs
  GitHub's App on another account, replaces a forge's key or tokens,
  forgets a managed forge's admin's token and removes a forge: the
  FORGE requests (`engine.md`; `forge/deployment.md`). A pasted value rides its request as jig's carried
  value, sent once and kept by neither the client nor the core (3.6).
  A connect's or an install's link is printed, never opened (`line.md`, 5).
- **One crate of records.** `temper-client-protocol-wire` carries the
  forge family's records: the adoption, its outcome and the small
  store's record of a pending one (`forge/records.md`), the FORGE requests and their
  outcomes (`engine.md`), and those of `forge/served.md`. temper's
  server decodes them before the engine's root, which locates the
  repository and adopts it on the person's behalf, or runs the connect
  (`engine.md`).
- **One view, every shell.** `temper-client-view` builds temper's pages
  as skein-ui's tree (skein's `ui/README.md`); the terminal prints that tree
  with skein's text shell (skein's `ui/text.md`); the browser, its
  second shell, maps it with skein's browser parts (`web/README.md`).
- **A terminal first.** `temper-box open` runs temper's client, its view and
  skein's text shell over `jig-client-shell-native`, jig's native
  transport (`line.md`).
- **Signed in by jig's printed link,** on loopback, and kept signed in
  across restarts by its bearer credential, which the terminal keeps in
  the client's private directory (section 4). A native client, the
  TUI, may instead be approved by the person's browser, signed in
  there, on the approval page (`pages/README.md`, `README.md`, 4.1). The forge as people's
  sign-in provider is open (section 5).
- **temper's server owns the clients' listener,** and mounts jig's
  clients' component on it with jig's default HTTP mapping, under
  `/jig`, with its example guards, and routes `/forges/...` for a
  GitHub connect's browser steps (`engine.md`; `forge/served.md`, 1). The
  mapping's engine end
  owns its calls in flight: the server asks for its room before it
  hands it a call. An ordinary call under `/jig` that finds none waits
  for it, its body unread (`web/README.md`, 2.2); once the server is
  stopping it answers that call itself, as it answers a path no one
  claims: `503`, with no body, closing the connection (jig's
  `protocol/client/default-http.md`, 4). A public read that finds none takes its
  own busy answer (17.5 there). The server's connections are its own
  bound, never the engine end's.
- **Joined to the engine from the start.** `tests/client`
  (`temper-client-world`) runs temper's client and view, with skein's
  fake person, against temper's engine root (`testing.md`;
  `../testing/checks.md`, 3).

# Tools: temper for agents

Provisional, 2026-10-17, revised 2026-11-05. How agents drive temper:
they read what it holds, act through jig's operations and wait for what
changes, with `temper ctl`, a command line. It serves any agent working
with temper for a person, which reaches the systems temper works with,
the forge first, by tools of its own choosing (`tools.md`, 2). QA's passes after
landing are one such agent (`../../../development/qa.md`). The tools'
bounds are section 9, and what they take of jig section 10; an MCP endpoint and the tools' extraction into jig are later
(section 11). What is still open is listed in section 12.

## 1. In one page

- **Agents drive the plumbing, not the pages.** The tools are jig's
  reads, watches and operations (jig's `client/domain/contract/README.md`),
  jig's helpers where they fit (`shapes`), and temper's forge family.
  An agent never reads a page, a position on a screen or a layout
  (section 3).
- **Each result says what can be done next.** jig's documents carry the
  acts the party may make (made, proposable, refused); every result
  prints them as the calls that make them, with the revisions they name
  (`results.md`).
- **One table of tools.** Each tool is defined once: its name, its
  arguments, its result, its text. The parser, the help, the JSON and
  the tool names the pages may use are derived from it (`tools.md`, 1).
- **A wait is a read.** A read of a watched subject, given `--since` or
  `--until`, blocks until the subject changes from what the agent saw,
  or meets a condition, or a timeout passes (`tools.md`, 4).
- **Each request once.** A tool that makes something requires a key the
  agent names; asked again under that name, it gets the first answer,
  never a second chat. Acts that decide name the revision the agent saw
  (section 5). The named key is jig's (section 10).
- **A turn too large for a page is read alone,** by jig's read of one
  turn (`tools.md`, 2, `README.md`, section 10).
- **Thin command line.** `temper ctl DIRECTORY GROUP VERB ...` makes one
  call per process, in `jig-client`'s one-shot mode, over jig's native
  transport, and prints its result (section 6).
- **The deployment under test is a box** (`../../protocol/setup/box.md`
  ): the agent's harness starts it, signs in by its printed link, adds
  the deployment's LLM key, and throws it away (`README.md`, section 8).
- **LLM accounts are an area of their own** (`areas.md`, area 5): a sign-in
  prints the address to open and returns, and the agent follows it with
  `account list --until settled`; a key is read from a private file and
  sent in a request that is never kept (section 5).
- **Forges are an area of their own** (`areas.md`, area 6): `forge list` reads
  each forge the store keeps as the `Forges` page shows it; a set up or
  a connect without FORGE makes one, and `forge remove` removes one; a
  connect prints
  its link as text, never opened, or reads its pasted value from a
  private file, and returns, and the agent follows it with `forge list
  --until settled`; `forge repositories` reads the picker's page, whose
  rows ride `repository adopt`.
- **Grown by areas,** the first, the fifth and the sixth designed in
  full, the others named (`areas.md`); **later, named:** the MCP endpoint and
  the extraction into `jig-client-tools` (section 11).

# The forge in temper's client

Provisional, 2026-10-05, revised 2026-11-03. What the forge connector
adds to temper's client: a change's way to landing and its result, a
project's landing queues, approvals at a head, the forge's holds in
words, repositories with their health and landing rules, the picker of
repositories to adopt, goals, findings and landings as the forge shows
them, and the forge's part of the deployment, where a forge is set
up, connected or removed. jig's pages and cards (jig's `client/domain/pages.md`
and jig's `client/domain/objects.md`) leave a slot for each part a
connector shows (jig's `client/domain/root.md`, section 6), and
temper's pages and cards, its own after them, show the same parts
(`../README.md`, section 1); this document says what the forge shows in
those slots and on the pages only temper has, `Changes`, `Adopt` and
`Forges` (`../pages/README.md`), in temper's words. The forge's client
family, its words for resources and kinds, its adoption form and its
requests are `family.md` and `../engine.md`. Each part shows what `../../domain/forge/README.md` keeps or reads,
and links to the forge for code. What is still open is listed in
`README.md`, section 8.

## 1. In one page

- **In jig's slots, in the forge's words.** jig's pages show tasks,
  cards and holds; the forge fills the parts they name (a panel, a
  result, an effect, an outbox entry, a hold, a project's part, the
  deployment's part) on a task's page, in a chat, in the inbox, on a
  project's pages and on the deployment's (`served.md`). A part the
  forge's side does not read says "Shown on the forge." (`family.md`,
  2).
- **The first milestone needs two of them** (`../../README.md`, section
  2): a change's result and its hold, in the forge's words, with a link
  to each on the forge (2.5).
- **A change is shown as its way to landing,** one step at a time, each
  with its reason in words: CI running, a review asked for, second in
  the queue, updating from main, resolving a conflict.
- **Queues are shown, not managed.** A landing branch's queue is its
  ready changes in order, on temper's `Changes` page; a person changes
  the order through goals' priorities on the board, never by moving
  changes about. Where a branch lands through GitHub's merge queue,
  temper's queue decides only what enters it and when, and the panel
  says "in GitHub's merge queue, n" (2.1).
- **Code stays on the forge.** Parts link to a pull request, its diff,
  its files and its failing jobs; the client shows states and verdicts,
  never code, and has no code review of its own.
- **Approvals are at a head,** given in the client as a person task or
  on the forge as a review (section 4): each counts at the head it was
  given at, and the panel says when a later head still carries it.
- **A hold says what happened on the forge,** and what each act would
  do; nothing on the forge is written over until the person decides
  (section 5).
- **What the forge did not tell temper is said:** a repository with no
  webhooks, protection temper could not read, a branch someone else
  moved, a job's log on a host temper does not read (`deployment.md`).
- **Forges are made and connected from the client.** A forge is a
  record the store keeps, made by the set up or the connect that brings
  it (`../../protocol/forge/engine.md`, 1 and 2); no configuration names one.
  On the deployment's `Forges` page (`deployment.md`) and its flow, a person whose
  role holds the deployment's connectors sets up a Forgejo on this
  machine, or connects GitHub through an App made from temper's
  manifest, or one of their own, its key pasted, or a Forgejo of
  theirs with a site admin's token from which temper makes its bot.
  The page lists the forges kept, says what temper is there and what
  stopped it, offers Replace, and removes a forge, confirmed, saying
  what goes and what stays on the forge.
- **Adoption picks from what temper can reach** (`repositories.md`, 1): per connected
  forge, the repositories temper can reach there, a page at a time by
  number, and "Not listed" for one named by owner and name, with what
  would make it reachable. A forge not connected offers none.
- **The forge's refusals are said with their remedy** (`repositories.md`, 4): not
  connected, not installed on an account, not granted to temper's App
  (linked to the installation's page on GitHub), stopped and why.
- **The browser's steps of a connect are temper's routes** on the
  clients' listener (`served.md`, 1): the manifest's page, GitHub's redirect and
  its setup, each authenticated by a one-use state alone. A browser's
  tab goes to a connect's link and comes back to the page that sent
  it; the terminal prints the link and never opens it.

# temper's pages

Before its document arrives, a task page says “Reading task N…”, or one
sentence explaining a lasting refusal; after a transient read end it asks
again while open, waiting one second, doubling to at most thirty seconds.
A conversation's activity reads, a chat's and an agent's task's, follow
the same rule: a read that ends transiently (busy) is asked again from
the position it asked for while the page is open, on the same waits, so
a committed reply always arrives without a further delivery to prompt it
(jig's `client/domain/client/README.md`: a read is one attempt, and its caller
asks again).

No page claims empty until its read has answered. "Nothing needs you.",
"No chats yet.", "None.", "No updates." and "No goals yet." stand only on a
list whose read has answered with its value. A read that ends without one
(busy, unreachable, refused, past a bound) is no answer, and neither is a
watch that has delivered nothing, whether it is opening or Behind. While a
read hasn't answered, on first load or after the link comes back, a page
keeps what it last knew, rows and words alike, through every refresh; with
nothing known, a section draws its title with no line under it.

Each page has an address, and watches what it shows while it is open;
leaving it closes its handles. Its mockup designs what temper does not
draw yet: its look, its parts and their order, its words and where its
acts lead (`../web/mockups/README.md`, section 1). Lists of tasks are
task queries (jig's `client/domain/contract/README.md`, 4.1): the chat and
goal templates' labels are jig's defaults, which temper's configuration
keeps (`../../protocol/configuration/README.md`, section 2).

# A chat

## 1. Its frame

- **One layout for every chat** (the greenfield mock's `chat.html`): every
  chat and `NewChat` take the layout of Ask temper's page
  (`temper-session`; `../ask-temper.md`, 1, *Ask temper's page*). The rail stays
  on every chat: the user's choice in `configure-chat-in-settings`, which
  supersedes the mock's rail-less chat. On a phone, at 760 px or less, rail
  and tab bar go, but for the rail's night switch, at the chat's bar's right
  (`../../view.md`, 3), and the tab bar of Ask temper's page and of an Ask temper chat, the
  phone's Ask tab (`../ask-temper.md`, 1, *Ask temper's page*). A chat differs from an Ask temper chat only in its path: a
  chat's path is "PROJECT / Chats /", "‹" on a phone a
  link to `Chats`; an Ask temper chat's is "PROJECT / Ask temper /", "‹" on a
  phone a link to `Ask { project }`, the rail's "Ask temper" current.
- **A chat's bar** holds its path, its level-1 heading named "TN · TITLE", the
  chat's task number and its title (a chat's first message, whole), and at its
  right, in this order:
  1. *Its number:* "T4", the same pixels as the mock's `.byline`. The browser
     alone draws it, as the terminal's heading says it; on a phone it goes.
     The model and effort are the composer's, and the account leaves the bar
     (`composer.md`, 1, *The work's account and model*). An Ask temper chat's is a chat's,
     then " · Ask temper", in the look of its tag in Chats.
  2. *Its status,* quiet: 12 px, faint, in lower case by the look alone.
     It is the colour of `.state-needs` while it waits for the person.
     A run that can't start says so ("Waiting for an account"), that colour
     too, and has no dots: the dots say a run is working, never that it
     can't start (jig's `client/domain/client/helpers.md`, 1).
     Every ended chat says "Closed", with no time: its end's line has the time
     (`../../README.md`, 3.6, *Closed ends on one line*).
     A chat the person paused says "Paused", quiet; one held for any other
     reason says "Held", and its card says why (1, *A person's pause*).
  3. *Pause or Resume, and Close,* while allowed, each quiet and small. The
     mock has neither; they are the only way to pause or end a chat.
  4. *Its own spend,* "N spent · of B", in the view and hidden in the
     browser.

  The bar's right part is a flex row.
  After the number, once a chat has a document, its mode group; with none,
  "+ Draft"; then "Start goal…" or its goal's link; on an Ask temper chat
  "Start over" (`document.md`, 1).
- **A chat's heading is short** (the request `chat-title`): it shows its
  title's short form, which a browser draws alone and the terminal after
  "TN · ". The short form is worked out in the view, in one function
  (`pages/chat.rs`):
  - *its words:* the title from its first character that isn't white space
    (a space, a tab, a carriage return or a line feed), up to its first line
    feed or through its first sentence's end, whichever comes first, with the
    white space at its end dropped. A sentence ends at a `.`, `!` or `?`
    followed by white space or by the title's end, so a `.` inside a word
    ("`../README.md`", "lib.rs") ends none;
  - *its length:* at most 72 characters, counted as Unicode scalar values.
    Past that, it is cut on a word: the words before the last space or tab
    within its first 72 characters, with any `,`, `;`, `:` and white space at
    their end dropped, then "…", so that the heading is at most 72 characters.
    Where that leaves no words, a first word of more than 71 characters, it is
    cut within it after its 71st character;
  - *the whole title* stays in the heading's name, "TN · TITLE", and where
    the short form isn't the whole title it is the heading's hint as well,
    which a browser shows on hover (`skein/docs/design/ui/README.md`, 3.2); a title
    shown whole has no hint. The person's message, the task card and the stored title keep it
    whole. Only a chat's heading is short: an agent's page keeps "TN ·
    TITLE" whole.

  The path yields to the right part: the crumbs take `flex: 1 1 320px`, so
  the right part keeps the bar's row while the path has 320 px, and the
  heading's ellipsis takes up the rest. Below that the row wraps, and a
  phone keeps the heading above the controls.
- **A person's pause** is a chat held by a placed hold of that person's own,
  mode Stop, over the chat's own subtree: the chip's hold names it, not lifted,
  its owner the party reading (jig's `client/domain/contract/documents.md`, 6). Nothing
  else is a pause: a hold of another's, of a task above it, or with a reason of
  its own says "Held", with its card and why, and has no "Resume": the card
  keeps its acts.
  - "Pause" is `Ask::Stop`, while Stop is allowed and the chat isn't paused.
    "Resume" takes its place on a paused chat where Release is allowed, and is
    `Ask::Release` naming the hold's revision. Each is sent at once (`../../README.md`, 3.6). The
    status says it happened: an accepted Pause or Resume draws no status under
    the conversation; a refusal says why there.
  - **Sending resumes.** A message to a paused chat, where Release is allowed,
    is one act: `Ask::Compound` of its `Message`, then a `Release` naming the
    hold's revision (jig's `client/domain/contract/requests.md`, 4; jig's
    `domain/tasks/changes.md`: no message releases a task, and words to a
    task its party stopped come with a release). The composer's hint says so
    while it is paused, "Sending resumes this chat.", in place of its usual
    words. It commits whole or not at all: a refusal, the hold's revision moved
    or the words refused, leaves the chat paused and nothing in its inbox, and
    the composer says why as for a lone message. Any other chat's message is a
    lone `Message`.
  - An agent's task's Pause and Resume are these acts (`../task.md`, 4); a message to it
    resumes nothing (`../../README.md`, section 5).
- **A chat's status,** in its bar and on its line of the Chats
  page, is jig's, narrowed as the mockups' "Where each value comes
  from" says: "waiting on 1 delegate", "waiting on N delegates" while
  it waits on its delegates, N its chip's live delegates, as a goal's
  header says it (`../goal.md`, 2). On the chat page, the delegated tasks' documents

  counts even if the chat's document still lists it. It says "waiting on
  your words" while it waits on news and its wake policy wakes it on
  words (its document's `wake`), from
  the person signed in, who requested it; jig's word otherwise. While
  a question a delegate asked it waits in the person's inbox, jig's wait

  you". A line of the chats page reads the same wake policy, from its

  person's words or Close move on (jig's `domain/tasks/lifecycle.md`, 9). A
  chat whose host refuses its start says jig's "Cannot start yet", and
  the feedback above its composer says why (below); a line of the Chats page holds

  configured bound (jig's `Exceeded` hold) says jig's phrase for that

# The TUI

- `pages/README.md`: the TUI's state, shared words, foot and commands.
- `pages/sign-in.md`: signing in and renewing from the engine's link.
- `pages/launchpad.md`: the launchpad, its rows and walking what needs you.

## 1. The TUI: `temper [ADDRESS]`

The TUI (`../../draft/tui.md`) is a person's full-screen terminal client:
temper's client root, a view of its own and skein's full-screen shell
(skein's `ui/screen.md`), over the native transport `temper-box open` uses.
Its first pass (`../../draft/tui.md`, section 4) signs in from the local
engine's link file, with a pasted link or the browser's approval as
fallback (`../README.md`, 4.1); once signed in it draws, live, a launchpad, the commands
of the browser's chat terminal and a chat read as that terminal reads
it (`pages/README.md`). `temper-box open` stays the tests' line client
(`../line.md`, 9).

- **Bare `temper`** runs the TUI when stdin and stdout are both
  terminals (skein-shell's `terminal_modes` answers for descriptors 0
  and 1), at the address of the engine's link file its environment
  names, when the binary reads one there (2, *The engine's link, kept
  at rest*), else at the address its directory keeps (2), else at
  `http://127.0.0.1:8367`, the clients' port `temper-box init` prefers
  (`../../../help/reference/commands.md`). Otherwise it prints the line it
  printed before the TUI, `temper: no command; temper --help lists
  them`, and exits 1.
- **`temper ADDRESS`** names the engine's clients' listener,
  `http://HOST:PORT`, with or without a final `/`, HOST an IP address on
  loopback, read as `temper-box open` reads an address (`../line.md`, 1). A box whose
  port fell back to another is named by its address.
- **The link is never an argument,** as `temper ctl`'s isn't
  (`../ctl/README.md`, 6.1): the TUI takes it pasted on its sign-in screen
  (`pages/README.md`), and refuses it as ADDRESS.
- **One argument at most,** an address: a first word that is no
  address and no command is no command, as before
  (`temper: no command WORD; temper --help lists them`).
- **Started by a box, too:** `temper-box` in a terminal runs the TUI in
  front of its services, `temper-box internal tui --box http://HOST:PORT`,
  signed in by a link the box hands it (`box.md`).
- **Exit status:** 0 once the TUI closed (4); 1 when it cannot
  start, with one line on stderr:

  | Cause | stderr |
  |---|---|
  | ADDRESS given, stdin or stdout not a terminal | `temper: the TUI needs a terminal on stdin and stdout` |
  | ADDRESS not an `http://` address, or HOST not an IP address and port | `temper: not an address: ARG` |
  | ADDRESS a sign-in link | `temper: a sign-in link is never an argument: run temper http://HOST:PORT and paste it` |
  | HOST not on loopback | `temper: the engine's address must be on loopback` |
  | two arguments or more | `temper: invalid arguments` and the usage, `usage: temper [ADDRESS]` |
  | the directory unresolved or refused (2) | `temper: DIRECTORY: ` and what was refused |

  A panic aborts the process (fail-stop), the terminal left as it was
  found (4).
- **In the deployment's client,** `temper-client [ADDRESS]` is the same
  TUI by the same start (`../README.md`, section 2), its lines naming `temper-client`
  where the table above names `temper`: `temper-client: the TUI needs a
  terminal on stdin and stdout`, `run temper-client http://HOST:PORT and
  paste it`, and, bare with no terminal, `temper-client: no command;
  temper-client --help lists them`. Its directory, its `address` and its
  `sign-in` note are the same files (2), so either binary finds what
  the other kept, and it reads the engine's link file as the box's
  `temper-box internal tui` does (`../../protocol/binaries.md`).

  WebAssembly under a browser shell of temper's,
  `temper-client-shell-web`, on skein's browser parts (`skein-ui-web`;

  holds its bundle, so that running it is the whole install. The
  machine's builds, temper's gate and its agents, build without it

- **Browser tests only for what a browser alone shows.** temper's

  the bundle under its policy, signing in by the link and the cookie,

# temper's client, in mockups

Revised 2026-10-24, with the user. The mockups are the design of
temper's client's pages, as design documents are of its code: what
each page shows, in what order and words, and where each act leads, in
its intended form. `STATUS.md` says how far the client is from them,
and which pages are under the rules below yet.

## 1. In one page

- **The greenfield mock leads.** `../greenfield/` is the target for
  the client's look, flow and mechanics (the user, 2026-11-05); where
  it differs from a page here, it is the design, and this page is
  reconciled to it as the client lands.
- **Binding for what is not built yet.** temper is the source of truth
  for what it draws (the user, 10-26): where a mockup differs from what
  temper draws, the mockup is reconciled to temper, gradually, and a
  built page's look is tuned on its journey's print
  (`../journeys.md`, section 7). For what is not built yet, a page's
  look, its parts and their order, its words and where each act leads
  are its mockup's. The client's documents (`../../README.md`,
  `../../forge/README.md`, `../README.md`) hold what a page reads, watches and
  does, and cite the mockup for the rest. The two agree: a change to
  one changes the other in the same landing, and a disagreement found
  is settled, never recorded.
- **Realistic.** A page's main drawing shows only what the engine has
  today: the data the client can read and the operations it can call,
  so that building the page is the client's work alone. Each value
  cites where it comes from (section 3).
- **The future, beside it.** What would need work below the client (the
  engine, jig, a protocol, temper box) is drawn only under View →
  Future, each piece marked with what it needs. Once that lands, the
  mark goes and the piece joins the main drawing.
- **Page by page, state by state.** Every state of a page has an
  address of its own, and the contents page lists them page by page.
  Worlds hold the data that keeps the pages consistent; a journey is a
  world whose steps walk one path (section 4).
- **`STATUS.md` keeps the distance:** what a main drawing shows that
  the client doesn't draw yet, where the client does otherwise, and the
  pages not yet under these rules.
- **Brought under the rules a group at a time:** an audit, the user's
  review, then the drawing, the documents and `STATUS.md` changed
  together (section 5). The bar above each page says whether it is.

# The agent

Provisional, 2026-10-05, revised 2026-11-05. temper's agent is smith's:
the standard agent built from smith, the kit for LLM agents, on skein
like temper. smith's design is its own (smith's `domain/run.md`,
`smith/docs/design/domain/session.md`, `smith/docs/design/domain/tools.md`, `smith/docs/design/domain/host.md`), and how a
run's charter, tools, contract, messages and delivery become smith's is
jig's (jig's `domain/hosts/charters.md` and `jig/docs/design/domain/hosts/README.md`, section 7). This document says
what temper fills in: the charters its engine writes, the host tools its
engine serves (`tools.md`, the one table of them), its result contracts and
conventions, delivery as a push, and the agents temper runs. What is
still open is listed in `README.md`, section 8.

## 1. In one page

- **temper runs smith's stock agent,** on workers, one process per run:
  temper's own binary run as `temper-box internal agent`, smith's agent shell
  (`../../protocol/binaries.md`). temper builds no code into it. A
  run's charter and its host's answers are all that make it temper's.
- **The engine writes the charter** (`charter.md`): the task's instructions,
  its brief rendered, every host tool, a result contract with temper's
  verdicts and a change's `title` and `body`, `.temper/pre-pr` and
  `AGENTS.md` as conventions, and a budget in the deployment's unit with
  its models' prices.
- **The host tools are temper's** (`tools.md`): jig's core's tools under temper's
  own names, and the forge's reads, effect and topics.
- **The worker is smith's host** (section 5; ../worker/README.md): it prepares the
  workspace from the forge, supervises the agent's process with smith's
  host domain, delivers a change by pushing it, and relays the rest to
  the engine.
- **The engine keeps what smith leaves to hosts:** transcripts, turn by
  turn, in its store; host tools' calls, decided once; what follows an
  answer (jig's `domain/engine/executors.md`).
- **One translation.** `temper-engine-smith`, which the
  engine's routing calls, is the only place where temper's charter,
  tools and results become smith's, and smith's back. It uses
  `jig-protocol-charter` to assemble and translate the charter, supplying
  temper's host tools, workspace delivery fact and conventions (`charter.md`;
  jig's `domain/hosts/charters.md`). Its `Tool` vocabulary is its own,
  rather than `jig-protocol-tools`' (`../../protocol/llm.md`, sections 4
  and 5). smith interprets no temper vocabulary, and the worker none of
  either.

# The forge connector

Provisional, 2026-10-04, revised 2026-11-02. How temper drives a forge,
Forgejo or GitHub: the forge as a connector on jig's contract (jig's
`domain/connectors.md`), with its resources, effects, topics, procedures,
requirements and reads; a forge connected or not, and removed; adopting
repositories, and what the picker offers; keeping up; a change's way to
landing and the requirements its merge meets; landing queues, a forge's own merge
queue among them; updates and conflicts; issues; and the ways temper
takes part in what others own. Its protocol layer, the component that
makes each call to a forge and hears its deliveries, is
`../../protocol/forge/README.md`. What is still open is listed in section 11.

## 1. In one page

- **What the forge holds:** git; pull requests as the unit of a change;
  CI; branch protection, as a second net; and a record people can read.
  From what temper owns there, only CI, people's approvals and drift
  come back: no comment on it is an input, and no label marks what
  temper tracks.
- **Live work comes from the store.** The connector holds a working set
  of what the forge owns of the resources live tasks name (pull
  requests' heads and states, CI on those heads, landing branches'
  tips), kept up by listing what changed, sooner on a webhook's hint.
- **Quiet on the forge.** temper reads what a reader needs, when it can
  have moved (`keeping-up.md`, 2 and 3): a branch nothing has pushed
  waits on its push, with `fallback` as its only poll; a hint wakes only
  the changes it can concern; a gather reads each commit's CI once and
  shares a fresh read its fellows ask with it; the repository's own
  facts are read once and kept; pages hold as many real rows as their
  bytes allow; and a read of something unchanged costs a `304`.
- **The change procedure** takes a change from produced to landed:
  produce, open, CI, gates, its turn in its landing branch's queue, an
  update from its base when it lacks the base's tip, and a merge at
  exactly its head; CI and the update only where its landing branch's
  rules ask them (`change/gates.md`, 2). Agents are woken for what needs judgement: producing,
  repairs, reviews, conflicts.
- **Landing rules are requirements.** A project's rules say what a merge
  into a branch needs: CI passed, the head up to date with the branch,
  gates, people's approvals. The forge judges each from its own facts at
  the merge's exact head, which the forge's merge is conditional on (`change/gates.md`, 2).
- **By queue or by turn.** A project lands by the queue below, its
  default, or by turn: landings across repositories, prepared, proved by
  receipts and fast-forwarded in the project's turn (`../landing.md`),
  which replaces steps 3 and 5 to 7 of `change/README.md`, 2, and `queues.md` and `README.md`, section 6, for
  its changes.
- **Landing queues.** Changes ready to land in a branch wait in line, by
  their goal's priority then by when each became ready; only the first
  is brought up to date and merged. Where the forge keeps a merge queue
  of its own on the branch, temper's queue decides only what enters it
  and when; the forge lands it, or ejects it, which is a repair (`queues.md`, 1).
- **Connected or not.** A forge is connected while temper runs, or not:
  the root knows which from the routing's events, rebuilt at each start.
  A forge not connected answers every call `NotConnected` at once, and
  adopts nothing. Its writer, given when it connects, is the forge
  client's own record, restored at start (`adoption.md`, 1).
- **A forge can be removed,** whole, by the deployment's owners: ended
  at once, its live work cancelled, its secrets and records erased, its
  box group stopped and its directory removed, each act kept until the
  removal is whole; nothing on the forge is undone but what temper ran
  (`adoption.md`, 1).
- **Adoption picks from what temper can reach:** a GitHub App's
  installations, what a connected Forgejo's API token can push to, or
  any repository of a managed or owned Forgejo, where adoption first
  gives temper's user its access (`adoption.md`, 2, 3). What it reads of the landing
  branch's rules, and the merge style, it says in words (`adoption.md`, 4).
- **Updates are lazy and mechanical.** A change takes its base when it
  is first in line, about to get a run, or at its check with its head
  already behind (`change/README.md`, 2, step 3), through the forge's own update; an LLM
  only resolves a conflict, in a workspace where the merge is in progress.
- **Every landing is news** for every goal working in that repository;
  it wakes a goal's coordinator only when it overlaps the files of that
  goal's changes.
- **Issues stay, one per goal,** as a projection people can read: the
  goal, its plan as a task list, and its milestones. Pull requests refer
  to it.
- **Plugged in as it is.** temper adopts repositories with whatever
  access it is given; webhooks, labels, protection and CI are all
  optional. Nothing is forced: pushes are fast-forwards, and merges are
  made at exactly the head decided on.


=== temper/docs/design/domain/forge/change/README.md ===
# The change procedure

## 1. What a change is

A change task asks for one change to one repository, landed into one
branch. Its parameters:

- **the repository and the landing branch;**
- **the branch,** held by the task: one it is handed, already pushed (a
  chat's small fix, handed over by the chat that made it: jig's
  `domain/tasks/holds.md`, 4), or its own, named after its tree
  (`../branches.md`), which a producing task pushes; or, held by its
  requester and not by the task, its requester's held branch: the
  requester's own goal branch (`../branches.md`, 1), which the change lands whole
  (below);
- **what to produce,** when nothing is pushed yet: the spec of its
  producing task, and its producer's charter, which its repairs,
  resolutions and checks' gates run under too: the one `change` names,
  or the forge's change charter (`gates.md`, 3); or, for a catch-up, the commit
  the merge job merges into a branch made at its landing branch's tip
  (`../README.md`, section 6), parameter 16, its producer that job and no agent; or,
  for a goal's close step, the close request (`../../../protocol/tooling.md`,
  4.2), bytes parameter 17, its producer the close job, kind 4, run at
  a branch made at its landing branch's tip, and no agent (`../branches.md`, 2);
- **its agents' LLM choices,** by role (jig's `domain/tasks/README.md`, section
  3): the producer's, for its producing task, its repairs, its
  resolutions and its checks' gates; the reviews', for its agents'
  gates. Each is given as the LLM choice of every delegate of its role;
  a role without one leaves its delegates to their runs' checks;
- **its gates** (`gates.md`, 1);
- **`with`,** under its project's turn: the changes it must land with,
  in any repository and of any goal its requester may name
  (`../../landing.md`, 4.1);
- **the pull request's title and body,** or what to make them from, and
  the goal it is part of. The change's words are its spec, kept whole
  for its producer's brief and bounded by the task's own limits, never
  by the forge's. The producing task's successful result supplies the pull's
  explicit title, retained with that result and copied into the change before
  opening. A visible pushed branch waits for this metadata; it alone cannot
  open the pull. This wait counts the stall as the producing phase does (`repairs.md`). A
  failed producer supplies none, and holds whatever it pushed (`repairs.md`).
  The title is fitted to 256 UTF-8 bytes, preserving punctuation
  and marking a cut with “…”; it is never interpreted as sentences. A handed-in
  branch supplies its title as bytes parameter 10. A missing or empty title on
  that path uses “Update repository”, never context from the brief. An empty
  title reaches no forge by any path: a pull request opened with none takes
  that same default. The body
  holds the supplied body or the words whole, fitted to the forge client's
  operation bound and marked when cut. The complete production brief stays in
  the task spec, separately from the title and body. A goal's issue title uses
  its spec's first sentence, with the trailing full stop dropped and a cut at
  a word marked with “…”; a landing's name prefixes the explicit change title
  with “Land ”, fitted to its task bound without sentence extraction.
  A change is never refused or held for the length of its words;
- **its bounds:** repairs, resolutions, updates, and the stall of each
  wait;
- **its questions:** how it takes its agents' questions (`questions.md`, 1): `none`,
  the default, asking no one; `foreman`, each settled first by a foreman
  of its own; `up`, each passed straight to its requester; and how many
  it takes, a bound beside the others.

A run asks for one with `change`, giving the words, and the root fills
in the rest from the project (`../../agent/tools.md`, 1). A run whose authority
holds jig's control family names, too, what a plan decides: the landing
branch, its own goal branch or a branch of the repository; its
dependencies, task numbers or changes of the same call; several
changes in one call, made as one batch; its producer's charter; and its
agents' LLM choices; and its questions. The landing branch is parameter 2, the
dependencies are the task's own (jig's `domain/tasks/README.md`, section 4),
and a change made with neither is as a chat's. The producer's charter
and the LLM choices are one text parameter, 11, so that a change stays
within the deployment's parameters per spec (jig's `tasks.parameters`,
4 in small): `C;P;R`, `C` the producer's charter's number in decimal,
`P` and `R` the producer's and the reviews' LLM choices, each part
empty where the change names none. A choice is spelled as jig keeps
it, by configured index (jig's `domain/tasks/README.md`, section 3): its main,
then each sub-agent, separated by one space, each `M` or `M.E`, the
model's index and its effort's, in decimal (`6;3.1 0.0 2;3.0`). A
change without parameter 11, or with an empty part, runs its producer
under the forge's change charter, or leaves that role to its charter's
default; a parameter 11 that does not spell them refuses the change's
start.

Before building a change batch, the root reserves one task for each
member and shares the remaining descendant capacity equally, bounded
by each member's change policy: its task allowance, plus its question
allowance for `foreman`. Room beyond a member's cap is redistributed;
remainder slots go in call order. Each member needs at least one
descendant for its producer. One change keeps its policy allowance
within the requester's remaining authority. These allowances are
prospective within the call, not reservations retained across calls.
Admission remains atomic, with the same dependency and funding checks.
An insufficient batch names the available and minimum required task
capacity (two per member), and whether fewer changes per call can fit.
Below two available tasks, smaller calls cannot help. The requester's
remaining authority counts tasks already made over its life, including
ended tasks, rather than allowances granted by earlier calls.

Its agents hold the forge's reads (kind 1, `../README.md`, section 2) on its
repository, beside the kinds it makes (`../../agent/tools.md`, 1): a reviewer
reads its own pull request and its files, and a repair a failed job's
log, through the reads an agent may call, past what its brief carries.

A change made with questions `none` asks no one. What to change is
agreed before it is asked for, by a goal that asks first where its
person wants to agree a plan (`../../agent/agents.md`, 2), and the procedure
delegates its agents without the Message family. One made with
`foreman` or `up` relays its own agents' questions instead (`questions.md`, 1); what
follows describes `none`; an enabled route's fallback feedback follows 8.11.
A change with `none` takes no questions, answers or words, and says
so rather than drop one: at its next step, a question in its inbox is
answered, in the answer form to the question's number, that a change
takes no questions and that its requester is task R; a task's words are
answered with words saying the same, to that task; each reply commits
with the step that takes what it answers, so a retried step answers
once. A party's words stay unanswered, since a procedure's step
messages tasks alone (jig's `domain/tasks/messages.md`, 5): they are taken and
dropped, and the change's page says that a change takes no words. Its
amendments and cancels are its own (2, 4).

A goal across repositories is several change tasks, ordered by their
dependencies: skein's change lands before temper's that bumps its lock.

**A change on its requester's held branch** (`change`'s `"branch":
"mine"`, `../../agent/tools.md`, 1) is how a goal lands what it holds: the
goal's landing, a change into the base whose branch is the goal's own
(`../branches.md`, 1). What a goal's runs write needs no such change: they push it
straight to the goal's branch (`../branches.md`, 3). It is an ordinary change, but for
the branch:

- **No producing step,** as for a branch handed over: it starts at
  opening, its head the branch's tip as read then. Its pull request is
  the branch's into its landing branch, its title its words' first line
  (bytes parameter 10, fitted as a handed branch's title is; an empty
  first line takes that path's default), its body its words. Every pull
  request's body holds at most 65,536 bytes of them, whatever
  `forge.client.op_bytes` allows, since a forge refuses more: one past it
  is cut and says that the change's full words are on its task in temper.
- **Held by its requester, not by the change.** The change takes no
  hold of the branch and hands none: its requester holds it for its own
  life (`../branches.md`, 1). A goal's landing made by the goal's closer (`../branches.md`, 2) is on
  the goal's held branch, its requester's requester's: the goal holds
  it, and the closer, which holds none, makes no other change on it. The change writes it only through its own delegates (its
  repairs and its resolution) and the updates it asks for, each taking
  the branch's writer slot under its requester's hold as it writes
  (jig's `domain/tasks/holds.md`, 3), as a change's delegates write its own
  branch. Its requester's other delegates write it too, a change landing
  into it among them: each such move is the change's new head (3).
- **One at a time:** a second change on the same held branch, while one
  lives, is refused at its batch, naming the first.
- **Closing deletes nothing:** landed, cancelled or failed, the change
  leaves the branch its requester's, deleted or kept as its requester
  closes (`../branches.md`). Cancelled, its pull request is closed as any
  (4), and its requester may make another.
- **Beside the run that made it:** the goal's own runs push design to
  the branch while the change lives (`../branches.md`, 3). That doesn't stop the change,
  which steps at the head it reads (3).

The question route is number parameter 14: 1 for `foreman`, 2 for `up`;
absent means `none`. The root writes it only for `foreman` or `up`. Any
other value refuses the change's start as a malformed parameter
(2026-11-04).

The close request a goal's close step carries is bytes parameter 17, the
request's exact text form (`../../../protocol/tooling.md`, 4.2), within the close
kind's spec bound less the command's path; a value in another form, beside
parameter 16 or on a change with a branch of its own pushed or handed,
refuses the change's start as a malformed parameter. Its start is refused,
naming the setting, where the change's project names no `close_job`, and
naming the kind where its authority does not delegate to kind 4. Like a
catch-up's, its producer is the job: the change makes its own branch at its
landing branch's tip, the job's checkout, and a job that pushed nothing ends
the change with no change, as a catch-up already contained does (`../branches.md`, 2).

The red landing a change repairs is carried as bytes parameter 12, its merge
commit as 40 or 64 lowercase hexadecimal digits. The start keeps it with the
change; a value outside that text form refuses the start as a malformed
parameter. Without it the change names no red landing to repair
(2026-11-04).

# The worker

Provisional, 2026-10-04, revised 2026-10-29. What temper's worker does,
as a domain layer: its root, which composes jig's hub with smith's host
domain and temper's checkout; the items an assignment gives a run's
workspace; and the checkout, which is temper's own. The worker hosts the
runs of agent tasks (jig's `domain/engine/executors.md`). Its hub, a
hosted run's lifecycle, its failures, turns, facts and losing contact
are jig's (jig's `domain/hosts/README.md`); the agent it starts is smith's, as
../agent/README.md says, and the worker is smith's host (smith's `domain/host.md`).
The mechanics are those of skein's `../foundation/programming-model.md`. What is still
open is listed in `README.md`, section 10.

- `checkouts.md`: workspaces, preparing, delivering and saving, and the checkout's open questions.
- `jobs.md`: the worker's job capability, its kinds, commands and failure diagnostics.

## 1. In one page

- **A worker is an execution host,** jig's third shape: agents on
  workers (jig's `domain/hosts/README.md`, section 3). The engine decides what
  work exists and keeps it in its store; the agent does the LLM work. The
  worker prepares workspaces, starts agent processes and keeps them in
  bounds, pushes the changes they make, and relays everything else
  between a run and the engine.
- **The worker keeps nothing durable.** What must survive lives in the
  engine's store (claims, transcripts, results) or on the forge (pushed
  branches, saved work). A worker can be killed at any moment and any
  worker can take any run; its checkouts are caches.
- **It hosts runs, not jobs.** A run may finish once, or wait for
  messages, take them and park (smith's `domain/run.md`, section 6): it
  starts fresh or from its transcript, takes messages, makes calls, sends
  turns, and parks or ends (jig's `domain/hosts/hub.md`, 2).
- **Policy is data.** The worker interprets no workflow vocabulary: no
  task, role, verdict or tool names. An assignment says which
  repositories to check out and how, what to save, and the charter to
  hand the agent, which is smith's and which it carries as bytes.
- **Its one forge write is a git push:** a change its run delivered, or
  saved work. Push credentials stay in the worker's protocol layer; an
  agent never sees them. Everything else that touches the forge goes
  through the engine.
- **The checkout is temper's** (`checkouts.md`): git workspaces cached by
  workstream, a merge in progress as a place to start, a push that
  commits exactly the tree the run checked and is never forced, and no
  credentials on disk.
- **The rest is jig's and smith's:** admission, fenced attempts, turns
  and answers kept until the engine acknowledges them, losing contact and
  shutting down are the hub's (jig's `domain/hosts/hub.md`); the
  agent process, its channel, its watchdog and cancel then kill are
  smith's host domain's (smith's `domain/host.md`, section 4).
- **The domain is complete** (skein/docs/foundation/programming-model.md, section 4). A world of
  domains and fakes runs everything the worker does, with no protocol and
  no io (section 9).


=== temper/docs/design/protocol/README.md ===
# The protocol layer

Provisional, 2026-10-03, revised 2026-11-02. temper's protocol layer:
how temper composes jig's protocol layer with components of its own (its
forge's, its checkout's, its client's) into the processes of one binary.
jig owns the layer's shape (jig's `protocol/README.md`) and the services'
(jig's `protocol/service.md`); this document says what temper adds and
the crates it is made of. The mechanics are those of skein's
`programming-model.md` (sections 2, 4 and 9); the entities it carries are
those of `../domain/`. Each boundary of temper's own is designed in a
document of its own (section 2). What is open across them is listed in
section 11. The cluster, temper as several processes of one machine for
its tests, is `cluster.md`.

## 1. In one page

- **Translation, and nothing else.** The protocol layer turns bytes into
  the domains' entities and back, and decides nothing about the domain
  (programming-model.md, section 4). The domain decides deadlines,
  retries and what a failure means; the protocol layer runs the call, its
  timer and its one attempt.
- **jig's protocol layer, composed.** jig ships the components for its
  own vocabularies: the store, the workers' channel, LLM accounts,
  clients, configuration (jig's `protocol/README.md`, section 5).
  temper's services own them as children beside smith's, skein's and
  temper's own components, and route between them, io and the roots, as
  jig's reference service does (jig's `protocol/service.md`, section 2).
- **What temper adds:** the forge's protocol (skein's forge component,
  with its webhooks' listener, handed temper's values and routed to the
  root and the forge connector); the checkout's io
  (the git component); its parts' codecs on jig's channel; its families
  in jig's store; its git identities; its host tools' translation; its
  client (its wire, its client root and view on `jig-client`, its
  terminal); the clients' listener, on which it mounts jig's clients'
  component and serves its forge routes; the setup host, skein's, in the
  engine (forge/engine.md, 6); and the forge's setup's commands outside the
  services (setup/README.md). Each is a crate of its own (`components.md`).
- **Foreign protocols are stacks of skein's machines.** Forgejo's REST
  API and GitHub's are skein's forge kit, each a dialect of it, over
  skein's HTTP client and JSON (skein's `forge/README.md`); git's arguments in and text out are temper's decoders
  over io's processes. temper's records on jig's channel, in jig's store
  and on the client's wire are generated from schemas by skein's
  generator.
- **Standalone first.** `temper-box serve` runs the engine and one worker in
  one process, on one loop, joined by jig's in-process address; the
  worker's agents are child processes of temper's own binary (`temper-box
  internal agent`); a person's client reaches the engine on loopback;
  the forge is polled, and hinted by its webhooks on loopback when they
  are configured (section 6; forge/README.md, section 4). Hosts apart,
  webhooks from another host, the forge as a sign-in provider and the
  forge's client documents come after (section 11). The browser is a
  person's client on loopback too (`../client/web/README.md`).
- **The cluster, for tests.** The same `temper-box serve`, on a
  document of another shape, is an engine alone or a worker alone. One
  engine alone and its workers alone, each a process of its own on one
  machine, joined over loopback in plaintext, with the fakes as
  processes beside them, are the cluster that temper's process tier
  runs from one description (`cluster.md`). It is no
  deployment's shape: a box keeps its one worker inside `temper-box serve`.
- **The box's binary, `temper-box`:** `serve`, `internal agent`, `open`,
  `program-login`, the forge's `forge prepare`, `forge run`, the bare form, and the
  agents' `ctl`, which `../client/ctl/README.md` designs (`binaries.md`).
- **No retries below the domain.** A call goes out once and ends once,
  with the failure the domain's vocabulary defines (`components.md`).
- **The forge's load follows its changes.** The calls a pass makes grow
  with what changed on the forge, never with its history, its labels or
  the items temper holds, and the worlds count them by HTTP request
  (forge/README.md, section 5).
- **Secrets never reach a domain.** A domain names a credential by number
  and generation; values live in the protocol layer's tables and in jig's
  secrets' store: LLM accounts' as jig keeps them, and each forge's
  records under temper's tag, from which its access and its git identity
  are made (section 8; credentials.md).
- **Tested at every tier:** codecs and parsers against golden bytes and
  transcripts of real peers; the forge component and the git component in
  worlds of their own; temper's client joined to the engine's root; the
  one-process world on skein's simulator; the real binary with an owned
  Forgejo, its runner and real git; a live tier with a real model
  (section 10; `../testing/README.md`).

# Configuration

Provisional, 2026-10-14, revised 2026-11-02. What temper's processes
are told when they start: the one JSON document `temper-box serve` reads,
and the forge's setup's commands with it, with jig's parts as jig
defines them and temper's own members beside them; the private files it
names; the agent's configuration; what `temper-box open` and `temper-box
program-login` are given; limits; what holds at a first start and at
every start; and the formats an operator writes. A forge is not
configuration: it is a record in temper's store, made at runtime
(../forge/engine.md, 1), and its credentials are records in jig's secrets'
store (../credentials.md, section 2).
jig's shape and rules are jig's `protocol/configuration.md`; this
document says what temper adds. What is open is listed in `README.md`, section 9.

## 1. In one page

- **One document per `temper-box serve`,** in jig's shape: three top-level
  members, `engine` (jig's engine part), `workers` (jig's worker parts)
  and `application` (temper's members). Nothing else at the top level.
  The forge's setup's commands read the same document (section 6). Its
  members give its shape: standalone, or the cluster's engine alone or
  worker alone (section 2).
- **Three directories,** configuration, data and cache, by skein's
  arguments' rule: the document is always `temper.json` in the
  configuration directory, each path in it is typed by the directory it
  is relative to, and no secret sits in the configuration directory
  (`directories.md`).
- **Read once, before the loop, and strict.** Unknown, missing,
  duplicate or out-of-bound members refuse the start, naming the path
  (jig's `protocol/configuration.md`, section 1). A change is a restart.
- **jig's parts are jig's:** `jig-protocol-config` parses them; temper
  writes no parser of its own for them, and adds only the checks its
  shapes need (section 2).
- **temper's members,** under `application`: `machine`, `landing`,
  `charter`, `clients`, `sign_in`, `webhooks`, `demo`, `welcome` and
  `limits` for the engine (`engine.md`), and `workers`, one entry per
  worker (`README.md`, section 3).
  `webhooks` is the forge component's webhooks' listener, on loopback
  (../forge/README.md, section 4).
- **No forge is configured.** A forge is a record of the forge
  connector in temper's store, made by the act that sets it up or
  connects it, numbered next at runtime and never reused, its number
  keying its records (../forge/engine.md, 1 and 2). A first run's document
  names no forge.
- **The document keeps the machine's** (`engine.md`): the clients' and
  webhooks' listeners; `machine`, the places a box's owned Forgejo and
  its fake forge bind, which `temper-box init --box` chooses and the box
  holds from its start (../setup/box.md, 1), and GitHub's, github.com's unless it
  names a fake; `demo`'s fake LLM's port; and the limits, the forges
  kept among them as a count (`limits.md`). A forge set up or connected
  takes its place from `machine`, or from what the person gives.
- **`demo`, the playground's scripted model** (../setup/playground.md): the fake
  LLM the box runs, its port, its LLM provider, its key and its
  scripts; null for none. `temper-box serve` reads its key alone, at the
  playground's start.
- **`welcome`, whether the document shows the welcome** (`engine.md`),
  whoever wrote it: `true` from `temper-box init --box`, absent otherwise.
- **Not configuration:** forges, which people set up or connect at
  runtime; repositories, which people adopt at runtime; the branch
  namespace, which the root derives from the deployment's id; the forge
  writer's identity, which comes from each forge's connect and which
  skein's forge client keeps in the main store (../credentials.md, 2.1);
  a forge's credentials, which come from the client or the engine's
  setup host (../credentials.md, section 2).
- **No secret in the document,** and no forge credential in a file the
  document names. Each forge's credentials are records in jig's secrets'
  store under temper's tag (../credentials.md, 2.1). The private files the
  document names are jig's, a worker's secret and its digest, read at
  start, and the scripted model's key, read at the playground's start;
  an owned or fake forge's handover is in its directory (section 5).
- **LLM providers, never accounts.** jig's `engine.llm` names the LLM
  providers, written out whole: their endpoints, models and prices, and
  the sources each offers. Accounts are made at runtime from the client,
  and jig keeps them (section 2; ../credentials.md, section 3).
- **One format rule** for temper's typed members: member names are the
  Rust field names, and the parser follows the types (section 8).
- **Limits by name,** their numbers in `temper-service`'s limits module,
  overridden by dotted name. The forge component's (`renew_before`,
  `log_poll`, `log_catch_up`, `log_pages` among them) are given once for
  every forge, never per forge; the forges kept, `forge.forges`, and the
  repositories, `forge.repositories`, are counts for the deployment
  (`limits.md`).
- **Standalone shape:** exactly one worker, joined to the engine by the
  in-process name `workers`; the clients' listener, and the webhooks'
  listener when configured, on loopback, the clients' at a fixed port
  for a GitHub forge; the agent program is temper's own binary. `temper-box
  init` writes it, and every deployment is it.
- **The cluster's two shapes,** for tests on one machine
  (`../cluster.md`): an engine alone, with no worker and a workers'
  listener on loopback in plaintext, and a worker alone, with no engine,
  dialling it. A document of none of the three is refused (`README.md`, section 2).
- **The agent's configuration is smith's** (section 4), and `temper-box open`
  takes no file at all (section 7).
- **Settings leave the document** (`settings.md`):
  jig's charters, templates, policies and rules, and temper's landing
  rules and forge's charter, become settings in the store, changed by a
  decision an owner accepts, with the binary's stock values as their
  defaults. The document keeps what a start needs before it reads its
  store, and what belongs to the machine.


=== temper/docs/design/protocol/forge/README.md ===
# The forge's protocol layer

Provisional, 2026-10-03, revised 2026-11-02. How the engine reaches a
forge, on skein's forge kit: what temper's forge component, its setup's
host and its forge connector do with it, and what the kit leaves to
temper. The kit is skein's (`forge/README.md`, `skein/docs/design/forge/connection.md`,
`skein/docs/design/forge/client.md`, `skein/docs/design/forge/setup/README.md`, `skein/docs/design/forge/github.md`,
`skein/docs/design/testing/fakes/forge.md`): Forgejo's API and GitHub's over HTTP and JSON, the
keys' markers written at the head of what the engine creates, the
webhooks Forgejo sends back to a listener on loopback and the deliveries
a GitHub App's log keeps, the client, the component, the setup and the
fake forge. A forge's credentials are records in jig's secrets' store,
loaded at start and handed over at runtime (`engine.md`; ../credentials.md,
the forges' records). The overview is ../components.md and ../README.md, section 4); the
vocabulary carried is skein's (`forge/README.md`, section 3), and the forge
client's use of it is `../../domain/forge/README.md`'s (`../../domain/forge/keeping-up.md` and `../../domain/forge/README.md`, section 4); the
machines are skein's (skein's `http.md`, `skein/docs/design/data/json.md`, `skein/docs/design/net/tls.md`). What
others owe this layer is in `README.md`, section 10, what temper relies on of each
forge in `README.md`, section 11, and what is open in `README.md`, section 12.

## 1. In one page

- **The component, the setup's host and the client are skein's, owned
  by temper.** `skein-forge-connection`, the forge component, and
  `skein-forge-setup`'s host run as children of the engine's protocol
  layer (`engine.md`, 6). `skein-forge-client`, the forge client, is owned by
  temper's forge connector (`temper-engine-domain-forge`). Each
  dialect's documents and call plans, Forgejo's and GitHub's, the keys'
  markers, the deliveries, the setup's plans and the fakes are skein's
  too (skein's `forge/README.md`, section 2; `skein/docs/design/forge/github.md`). This document
  holds what the kit leaves to its owner.
- **Translation only, and no retries.** Each call of the forge client (a
  `Read` or a `Write`) is answered by exactly one `Answer` or `Error`,
  made of one or a few HTTP exchanges on one connection. A call goes out
  once. Its answer says how many requests it cost, so the client's
  budget charges what the forge actually served (skein's
  `forge/connection.md`, section 1; `skein/docs/design/forge/client.md`, section 6).
- **temper's routing hands the component every value** (`engine.md`): the
  component reads no file. The values are each forge's records', in
  temper's store and in jig's secrets' store, and the configuration's
  machine and limits, with two of temper's own: the word `temper` and
  the user agent `temper/0.0.0`.
- **A forge is a record, made at runtime and removable** (`engine.md`, 1 to 3).
  The forge connector keeps each forge in temper's store: its kind,
  mode, place, git program, bot and a first set up's intent. `SetUp` and `Connect` make it, numbered next and never
  reused; no forge is configured. Its removal ends it at once: no call
  served, its access dropped, its secrets erased, and the box asked to
  remove its group (`engine.md`, 8).
- **Repositories by the forge's own id; paged, bounded, and decoded as
  it streams.** Forgejo's conventions are skein's (`forge/forgejo.md`, section
  1); the paths and the users the component keeps are
  `skein/docs/design/forge/connection.md`'s (`skein/docs/design/forge/connection.md`, section 4). Adoption first locates a
  repository by its owner and name (skein's `forge/forgejo.md`, 1.2).
- **The engine's marks are keys' markers only** (section 3). A creation
  carries its key at the head of its body. The word is `temper`, and the
  key holds the deployment's id as its namespace. The marker's form is
  skein's (`forge/README.md`, section 5). Nothing else of the engine's is
  written into the forge.
- **A forge starts connected or not, and stops alone.** Its access (an
  API token, or a GitHub App's id and key, and the user it must stand
  for) is built from the forge's records at start (`engine.md`, 1), or handed over
  by `Request::Connect` once a setup's run has kept them (`engine.md`, 2). A forge
  with none answers each call `NotConnected`. A forge whose credential
  is refused is stopped and named to the root, and the engine and every
  other forge run on; a forge removed is ended (`engine.md`, 3). The credential is the component's alone:
  never granted to a worker, never in the main store, and the first
  exchange with the forge checks that it stands for the forge's user
  (skein's `forge/connection.md`, section 3; ../credentials.md, the forges'
  records).
- **GitHub's installations and git's tokens come up as events** (`engine.md`, 4, 5
  ): the routing keeps the accepted installations in the forge's
  records, and makes each token the component mints a new generation of
  the forge's git identity, its value kept before jig's core hears of
  it.
- **Webhooks for latency, polling for liveness** (section 4). Forgejo's
  deliveries reach a listener of the component's own, on loopback, at
  `application.webhooks`'s address: verified as they stream, answered
  first, each hint naming what caused it (skein's `forge/README.md`, section 6;
  `skein/docs/design/forge/connection.md`, section 6). Polling stands as it is and finds
  what a delivery lost, refused or never sent told of; a forge on
  another host polls alone, since deliveries over TLS need skein's TLS
  server side (../setup/README.md, section 9). A GitHub forge's deliveries are
  read from its App's log every `log_poll` instead, with no secret and
  no guid kept across process starts (4.1).
- **The vocabulary carries what a forge connected at runtime answers**
  (`engine.md`, 7): `NotConnected` and `NotInstalled` at once, at cost 0;
  `RateLimited { after }`; the rate each answer reports; validators,
  whose `304`s are free.
- **The load rules' values are temper's** (section 5): the rules are
  kept in skein's crates, and `../../domain/forge/README.md` holds the values.
- **The fake forge, the throwaway and the conformance are skein's**
  (`skein/docs/design/testing/fakes/forge.md`). temper's worlds run temper's stories over them and
  count its costs (section 9).
- **What temper relies on of each forge is recorded.** Forgejo's by
  skein's conformance against a real Forgejo v17.0, each behaviour a
  named fact (skein's `forge/forgejo.md`, section 2). temper requires that
  release: one patch release of it is pinned (skein's `forge/README.md`, 4.3),
  and no other version is served. GitHub's are skein's `forge/github.md`
  facts, which temper's tests run against skein's fake alone (`README.md`, section 11
  ).


=== temper/docs/design/protocol/setup/README.md ===
# The forge's setup

Provisional, 2026-10-16, revised 2026-11-02. How a forge comes to be
ready for temper, in three modes: connected, where a person runs and sets
up the forge and gives temper only what it strictly needs; managed,
where a person runs it and temper sets itself up on it, with a Forgejo
site admin's token or, on GitHub, with an App made from temper's
manifest; and owned, where temper runs Forgejo and its runner, and sets
itself up on them. A fourth, fake, is the playground's: temper runs its
own fake Forgejo, seeded with the playground's repository, and sets
itself up on it as on an owned one (`playground.md`). A forge is a record of temper's
store, made by the act that sets it up or connects it (../forge/engine.md, 1 and
2); a person chooses a forge's mode and makes it from temper's client
(`../../client/forge/README.md`, the deployment's page), and may remove it
(`../../domain/forge/adoption.md`, 1). The
engine is the same in every mode: each mode makes the records the one
below reads, kept in jig's secrets' store (../credentials.md, the records),
and the engine's forge component reads connected's. Owned mode is
commands a box arranges, and `temper box` is wiring only. A box is what temper's first milestone is set up on and
checked by CI on (`../../README.md`, section 2). The members are ../configuration/README.md's (`../configuration/engine.md`),
the credentials ../credentials.md's (`../credentials.md`, section 2), the webhooks ../forge/README.md's
(`../forge/README.md`, section 4), and the commands and crates ../README.md's (`../binaries.md` and `../components.md`).
The walks are the pages `box`, `forgejo` and `github` (`temper help
NAME`). What comes later is `README.md`, section 9; what is open,
`README.md`, section 10; connecting GitHub, `github.md`.

## 1. In one page

- **Three modes, per forge** (section 2), chosen and connected from
  temper's client. Connected: a person runs and sets up the forge, and
  pastes the bot's two tokens on Forgejo, or an App's id and key on
  GitHub. Managed: a person runs it, and pastes a Forgejo site admin's
  token, or on GitHub makes temper's App from its manifest in their
  browser. Owned: temper runs Forgejo and its runner. A fourth, fake,
  is the playground's, made when the person tries it and never chosen
  as a mode: temper runs its fake Forgejo in place of Forgejo, with no
  runner and its git a route of its own, and sets it up as an owned one
  (`playground.md`).
- **Forges are records in the store** (../forge/engine.md, 1): each made by the
  act that sets it up or connects it, `SetUp` for an owned Forgejo,
  `Connect` for GitHub or a Forgejo elsewhere, `StartPlayground` for the
  playground's fake one, and numbered next when it is made, a number
  never reused (../forge/engine.md, 2). The document names no forge: it keeps
  the machine's, its listeners, what a box's owned Forgejo and fakes
  take from the machine, and the limits (`init.md`). A forge can be
  removed whole (`../../domain/forge/adoption.md`, 1); its box group is then
  stopped and its directory removed, the cache kept (`box.md`, 1).
- **Each mode is a layer that makes the inputs of the one below.**
  Owned's preparation makes a Forgejo and an admin's token, managed's
  input, and hands the token over in a file; managed's plans make the
  bot, its tokens, its id and the webhooks' secret, connected's inputs.
  The forge component always runs connected: it reads the same records,
  checks the same user, and never holds an admin's token (section 3).
- **The records are the interface.** Each forge's records, under its
  number, a stable id never reused, are kept in jig's
  secrets' store by the engine's protocol layer alone (../credentials.md,
  the records). The one file that passes between the layers is an owned
  forge's handover, `admin_token`, which the engine takes once.
- **The core is step code; the hosts are thin.** The setup's plans are
  skein's `skein-forge-setup` (skein's `forge/setup/forgejo.md`, section 1, and `skein/docs/design/forge/setup/github.md`, section 1
  ), run inside the engine by skein's setup host component (its host,
  `owned.md`, 3): per forge, one writing plan at once and the picker's read beside
  it (`reconcile.md`, 1). The preparation, Forgejo's settings and the processes' specs
  are machines and pure functions of `temper-forge-setup`, hosted by
  three commands, `temper-box forge fetch`, `temper-box forge prepare` and
  `temper-box forge run` (`owned.md`). No command writes the records.
- **`temper box` is wiring only.** It runs a configuration's steps in
  order (the fakes, fetch, prepare, Forgejo, its runner, `temper-box
  serve`), restarts what exits, and stops them in reverse order. It
  knows kinds, not forges: an owned Forgejo's group and the playground's
  fakes' wait, dormant, until `temper-box serve` asks over a pipe for one of
  them for a forge, and run again at each later start once started
  (`box.md`, 1). It names no Forgejo request and nothing of setup but its
  steps. systemd or a test arrange the commands another way, with no
  owned forge (`box.md`).
- **`temper-box init` writes a deployment that starts** into the three
  directories (../configuration/directories.md): the default document, its
  agent's configuration and the worker's secret; no forge, no account
  and no credential, which are the client's. With `--box` it adds the
  box's parts: the welcome's member, the owned Forgejo and the
  playground. `temper box` runs `temper-box init --box` first on an empty
  configuration directory (`init.md`, 2, 3).
- **A first run is empty** (`init.md`, 3): the store holds no forge, and, where
  the document shows the welcome (../configuration/engine.md), the
  person tries the playground or sets temper up. The playground is made
  when chosen (`StartPlayground`): a fake forge's record, its box group,
  the scripted account and the playground's project, into which
  `playground/widget` is adopted; its reset (`EndPlayground`) deletes
  the project and removes the forge and the account, and the person is
  on the welcome's Forge step as if they had never tried it. It is
  offered while no real forge is kept, and never once one is (`playground.md`).
  Real forges are made from the client: an owned Forgejo set up under
  the box, its group asked when the person sets it up (`owned.md`, 5, `box.md`, 1), and
  GitHub or a Forgejo elsewhere connected (`github.md`, `reconcile.md`, section 5); none needs
  a restart.
- **A set up writes as it goes, and starts over by removal** (`owned.md`, 7): the
  forge's record and its first repository's intent are kept as it goes;
  an unfinished forge found later is removed and set up again, Forgejo's
  binaries kept in the cache.
- **Reconcile converges at every start** of a managed or owned Forgejo,
  in the engine: the bot user, made when missing and otherwise checked,
  never edited; its two tokens; and each
  adopted repository's access, the bot a collaborator with write and, on
  a forge served the listener (`reconcile.md`, 3), the repository's hook; each made
  when missing, repaired when changed, and left alone otherwise (`reconcile.md`
  ).
- **Access at adoption.** Adopting a repository of a managed or owned
  Forgejo first gives the bot its access (`Adopt`, `reconcile.md`, 4); nothing is
  granted org-wide. The picker offers every repository the site admin
  can see, read by the `Reachable` plan, which writes nothing (`reconcile.md`, 2).
- **GitHub** (`github.md`): managed by the manifest flow, through the
  clients' listener's routes, or connected by an App the person pasted;
  both end in the same records. Its reconcile, after each connect,
  repairs only the App's hook's address; its changes reach the engine
  through the App's delivery log, with no webhook secret.
- **Owned is a stock Forgejo.** temper writes its `app.ini`, runs its
  migrations, makes its own admin and the person's account with
  Forgejo's command line, registers the runner offline, dumps before an
  upgrade's migration, and runs only the versions it pins, the
  conformance's, which it fetches itself when asked, checked against
  the digests it pins (`owned.md`). Its directory, once temper stops running
  it, is a Forgejo a person can run themselves, as managed.
- **A box is reactive.** Every change on the forge that a box's work
  waits on reaches the engine as a signed webhook delivery, and becomes a
  hint, read within the keep's `hinted`: pushes, pull requests, issues,
  comments, reviews and each CI job's end. Adoption and reconcile make
  the hooks; an owned Forgejo is allowed to deliver to the engine's
  listener, and a managed one's settings are the person's (`reconcile.md`, 6). Polling
  is unchanged, the fallback for a delivery lost or refused (section
  6).
- **Loopback.** An owned Forgejo listens on loopback, as temper's
  clients' and webhooks' listeners do; a person on another machine
  reaches them through a tunnel. A forge on another host polls: its
  webhooks need skein's TLS server side (section 9).
- **Not here:** webhooks from another host, the isolation of CI's jobs,
  provisioning at runtime and the forge as people's sign-in provider
  (section 9).

# Testing temper

Provisional, 2026-10-03, revised 2026-11-02. How temper is tested. The
strategy is skein's `../foundation/testing-strategy.md`: the tiers,
faults, what every fake shares, what every world checks, scenarios and
the referee, the two suites, and where a failure is fixed. This document
keeps what is temper's: its tiers (section 2), its neighbours and their
fakes (`README.md`, section 3 and `fakes.md`), its scenarios, referee and its case library
(`README.md`, section 4), what its worlds check beyond the strategy (`checks.md`), its
layout (`README.md`, section 5), and what is still open (`README.md`, section 6).

## 1. In one page

- **Behaviour is tested in the domain layer.** The engine and the worker
  are each a root domain with child domains, and each has worlds of its
  own; the system world runs the real engine and worker together, with
  smith's agents.
- **Below the domain, one tier per layer it adds:** step tests of every
  codec, translation and parser, against golden bytes and transcripts of
  real peers; machine worlds of the forge component, the git component
  and the terminal shell; the forge's protocol world; temper's client
  joined to the engine's root; the one-process world on skein's
  simulator; the real-loop world on one ring; reconcile's world; the
  process tier, the real binary with an owned Forgejo, its runner and
  real git; and the live tier, with a real model.
- **Peers across a wire, the machine beneath.** LLM providers, their
  sign-in's issuers and the forge are peers, whose fakes grow into
  services and meet temper at the wire; Claude Code, the program smith's
  agent runs on a program's login, is faked by smith's fake `claude`
  (`fakes.md`, 1); people use temper's client, and their fake is skein's fake
  person, in front of it. The machine (files, processes, and the
  programs they run: git, rg, sh, temper's own) stays one set of state
  machines, with a face for each layer of temper it can sit under.
- **The fake forge speaks Forgejo's dialect and, through its GitHub
  layer, GitHub's** (skein's `testing/fakes/github.md`, section 1), one world per
  forge kind, API and git in one store, so a branch the worker pushes is
  the head the engine reads. Its protocol layer serves the dialect's
  API; its git is reached by the fake git program, through the forge's
  own calls, never git's protocol over HTTP.
- **Checks follow content.** The machine's sh is a small subset of POSIX
  sh, in which fake repositories write their checks (`fakes.md`, 3), and the fake
  forge's CI follows a cue in the pushed tree (`fakes.md`, 2), so a change that is
  wrong fails in the run and on the forge alike.
- **A referee in every engine world,** on skein's world harness, holds
  its scenarios' expectations (4.2).
- **The case library runs in the real loop,** step by step, on its cast,
  its scripts and an oracle over effects that every world running the
  case reuses (`reference-case.md`).
- **No fake of temper's own components:** the system world runs the real
  engine and worker.
- **Two suites, and two tiers outside them.** The focused and the fuzzy
  suites keep their 30 s and 180 s, which nextest enforces; the process
  and live tiers run by their scripts, never at the gate. The process
  tier holds only what processes show, within its 540 CPU s (`process-tier.md`). The
  180 and the 540 are the strategy's 120 and the tier's 6-minute aim,
  raised by half by the user's word of 11-08, for now, to be revisited.
