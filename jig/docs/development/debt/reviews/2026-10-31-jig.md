# jig code-quality review, 2026-10-31

Scope: the whole `jig/` tree of fabrig at `main` = `c30cf6a03` (2026-10-31): 30 crates, 24 test kits, 26 test suites and the `ops` example. That is about 644k hand-written lines; the 1.41M lines of generated codecs were left out of the review itself. The rubric covered cleanliness, organisation, taste, naming, primitives (orthogonality and decoupling), elegance, economy and complexity, applied to tests as well as code. Correctness was out of scope; bugs seen in passing are collected in section 6.

How it was done:
- One holistic reviewer looked across the tree. Then 12 area reviewers each took code and tests together.
- All of them worked on a frozen snapshot, statically, with skein's programming model as the frame. Its constraints (no traits, generics or closures in step code, and so on) were treated as givens, not defects.
- Each reviewer formed its own view first, then cross-checked against `jig/docs/development/tech-debt.md` (last refreshed 2026-10-18) and 12 hypotheses from the holistic pass.
- I spot-checked the headline claims myself (section 9). Figures are the reviewers' measurements with `rg`/`wc` on the snapshot. Line "wins" are estimates and overlap between findings.

The condensed per-area reports are in the appendix (A0–A12).

---

## 1. Verdict

**At the small scale, jig is careful code.** Every match is exhaustive, every `expect` names its invariant, arithmetic is checked, and refusals are typed values. Modules cite the design, state machines carry their transition tables, and there are no TODO or FIXME markers. The step-code subset mostly holds.

The best parts are exemplary:
- authority's value and preorder (`order.rs`, `numbers.rs`), tested against a finite model;
- fleet's `attempt.rs` table with derived state;
- the store's state machines, with refusal words compiled from `store.md`;
- `jig-client-protocol-http`;
- the conformance world's fault catalogue.

**At the large scale, the tree has accreted rather than been designed.** It grew from 457k to 644k hand-written lines (+41%) in the 13 days since the last tech-debt refresh, and almost every measure that refresh took has got worse. The growth is not mostly behaviour. It comes from six things:

1. **Things kept after they stopped being used:**
   - 19 generated codec versions no crate compiles (1.12M lines);
   - a second client-document vocabulary the engine never sends;
   - a per-fragment sizing system with no production caller;
   - a `Request::Decided` no root reads.
2. **Transitions started and not finished.** There are two start/restore interfaces, threaded through the core, four children and the store. Production uses the "old" one; the new one is exercised only by tests.
3. **The contract written out by hand four or five times:**
   - the schema;
   - the engine's translation (10.4k lines);
   - the client domain's mirror (7k);
   - the link's translation (13k);
   - a test oracle (15k);
   - and the same again in the store codec (about 9.7k structural lines) and in ops.
4. **Decisions in the wrong layer.** The design's central rule is that a root only routes and translates, and that jig owns its invariants. It is broken in many places:
   - the reference root, which is about half jig wiring;
   - the charter policy, copied into every root;
   - the accounts component, written three times outside jig;
   - preparation ordering, done by every root;
   - the store codec, which applies domain rules;
   - the view, which sorts and merges facts.
5. **Primitives duplicated instead of shared:**
   - the worker-link vocabulary three times;
   - the Authority shape walked seven times;
   - the operation vocabulary written four ways;
   - limits copied between layers in nearly every area.
6. **Tests that check projections instead of the shipping code.** The client's joint world runs a hand-written projection, not the clients' component. The scripted engine speaks a dialect the engine never sends. No story runs on two fronts.

**Taste is good in the small and missing in the large.** The weakest dimensions everywhere are economy and primitives. The fixes are mostly deletion and consolidation, not new design. Two of them need a generator in skein.

A rough size of what could go: on the order of 100k hand-written lines (about a sixth), with no loss of behaviour or test confidence, plus 1.12M generated lines. Section 7 orders the work.

## 3. What is good (keep it, copy it)

- **Layering holds mechanically.** Every `jig-domain-*` crate depends only on `skein-lib` (accounts and fleet also on `jig-domain-workers`). The core links no smith, and all non-shell crates are `no_std`.
- **One step shape:**
  - `step(domain, env, event, out)` in 13 domain crates;
  - `valid(&Limits)` in 23 crates;
  - one `Limits` per crate;
  - worlds in one layout (`world`/`referee`/`fixtures`/`stories`).
- **Exemplary modules worth copying:**
  - authority's `order.rs` and `numbers.rs`;
  - fleet's `attempt.rs` (a state × event table, with implied state derived in one `follow`);
  - views' `watch.rs`;
  - core's `projections.rs` and its restart script (`restart.rs:282-351`);
  - tasks' compound check/apply `Plan` with a staged overlay;
  - the worker wire's data-driven `KINDS` table;
  - worker-link's `connection::Phase` holding data per state;
  - `jig-client-protocol-http`;
  - the client domain's step machine;
  - ops' connector state machines.
- **Design-to-code-to-test traceability:**
  - the store's refusal words are compiled from `store.md`;
  - `engine.md` 4.4 cites, by name, the world tests that hold its ordering;
  - the conformance world's ten named boundary corruptions match `testing.md` §5 one for one.
- **Referees that judge from outside.** The core world reuses the conformance referee over store rows and released outputs. The client referee reads surfaces only. The protocol world checks "stored before sent", "a carried value is never kept" and "no secret reaches a domain".
- **Secret hygiene:** no `Debug` on token-carrying types, and secrets typed as `Secret`.

### T2. Unfinished contractions: two of each

- **Two start/restore interfaces.** Core has `start_begin` beside `restart_begin`. Tasks, people, authority and accounts each carry two restore paths, and the store carries a branch with 11 forks. Each child has its own once-only mode selector under a different name:
  - `RestoreMode::{Admission, LoadFitLink}`;
  - `StartMode::{Default, LoadAndLink}`;
  - `start::Phase::{Default, Loading, …}`;
  - `Start::{Default, Loading, Linked}`.

  Both production roots and temper call `restart_begin`; the new path runs only in tests. Records are validated twice with different semantics: accounts' default path drops a misfit silently, while the explicit path refuses it. The tasks path the status file calls "legacy" survives because it avoids the word the boundary world bans. (A0 F3, A1 F20, A3 F1, A4 F1–F2, A6 F14)

  **Win:** 3–5k production lines, about 3k test lines, and four concepts become one. The choice of which path survives belongs to the designer (`engine.md` 6 describes the new one).
- Two application roots of different shapes (A0 F14, A11 F3), and two worker entrances into the test root (A11 F5).
- Two vocabularies in the test family (`Item`/`Part`/`Touch` vs `Connector`) (A10 F13), in ops (`Native*`, T1) and in proposals (task vs person proposal families, A3 F4).

### T3. The contract, written out by hand four or five times

One record family is described:
- in the schema (about 1k lines);
- in `jig-protocol-engine-clients/src/translate/` (10.4k: 648 `…Parts {`, 466 one-line enum arms);
- in `jig-client-domain/src/contract/` (7k: 420 of 443 types named after a wire record);
- in `jig-client-protocol-link/src/translate/` (13k, at least 57% plainly mechanical; 432 `optN` locals; files split by size into `documents_part0..8`, `pages_part0..8`, `facts_part0..2`);
- again in `tests/client/src/joint/wire/` (15k, a test oracle).

The same pattern appears in the store codec (about 66% of 14.7k lines structural, with 947 one-line mirror arms) and in ops' `values_1..4.rs` (3.55k). (A0 F5, A6 F1, A7 F3, A8 F2, A12 F9)

The same holds for limits. The client's knob → `w::Limits` mapping is written in four places and recomputed on every decode (about 550 assignments per document decode). Each limit's name is written 4–5 times in config (A7 F14, A8 F3).

**Do:**
- **Short term, in jig:** a skein-codec piece reader; a per-record `to_bytes`; one `take` with `let`-`else`; files split by record family, not by number.
- **Long term, in skein:** give skein-codegen a "mirrors" declaration that emits the owned domain-side types and both translations, plus a limits mapping keyed by bound classes declared in the schema.

**Win:** 30–45k hand-written lines across engine, link, client domain, store and ops, plus the 15k oracle once T6 lands. This is the one theme that needs skein work first, as AGENTS requires.

### T4. Decisions in the wrong layer: roots and protocols that decide

The README's headline promise is "the code an application writes around jig only routes and translates". The design's rule is that jig owns every invariant. Both are broken in many places:

- **Roots:**
  - **The reference root (ops) is about half jig wiring:** about 2,100 of 4,524 lines are hub↔fleet relay, payload tokens, charter assembly, the core's restore protocol and timers. `jig-test-domain` holds a copy (A12 F1, A11 F4).
  - **The root decides:** a close grace taken from "the first conversation template", the served chat inferred from the requester chain, infrastructure's holdings, a backoff RNG, and policy as bare numbers (A12 F2, F4).
  - **Every root orders core-internal steps.** Each turns `StartPreparation` into `Prepare` then `StartBrief`, and drains `core.adopted` by hand (A1 F3).
  - **Every root drives the core children's restore,** dispatching each record family to `restore_task_row` + `tasks::step` and so on (A12 F3).
- **jig components written outside jig:**
  - **The charter translation:** `hosts.md` §8 promises it "in one place, `jig-protocol-charter`". That crate is a 1:1 mirror type. The translation and its policy (`TextRule{max:1024}`, `checks_must_pass: true`, `inspect: false`) are copied in ops and in the test service (A7 F1).
  - **The accounts component:** the ordering behind "the engine alone refreshes, so no two processes race a rotating token" is written outside jig three times: temper, the test service (91% identical) and the world's model (A4 F3, A11 F6). About 2k lines of `jig-test-service` are copied, with drift, into temper (A11 F6).
- **Protocol layers:**
  - The clients' protocol layer answers a people-admission rule (`ProjectMismatch`) that `contract.md` 3.4 gives to people (A7 F2).
  - The store codec remaps model catalogues three ways, invents a `u8::MAX` "model gone" sentinel, copies a domain message literal to size a bound, and serves a domain-named write (`CopySettingsRevision`) (A6 F2, F5).
- **The client:**
  - The view sorts, merges and filters the inbox, and re-sorts spend the engine already ordered (A9 F6).
  - The scripted engine orders its inbox with the reference UI's own rules (A10 F5).

**Do:**
- A jig "engine link" part that owns the payload, host-call, assignment, brief and turn-backoff maps and the fleet↔host translation.
- `core.restore(record)`, `core.next_deadline()`, `core.header_due()`.
- The core sequences preparation itself; split `Event` into a public input and a crate-private `Work`.
- `jig_protocol_charter::assignment(run, brief, sources)` owning contract policy.
- A `jig_protocol_accounts::Component`.
- Domain functions for the store's remaps.
- `jig_client::facts::inbox_rows`.

**Win:**
- about 2k lines out of each in-engine-agent root;
- about 650 lines of charter;
- about 1.2k lines per accounts copy;
- `route.rs` from 1,769 to about 1,100 lines;
- most importantly, an `ops` that can be a template again.

### T5. Primitives duplicated instead of shared

- **The worker-link vocabulary is written three times**, in workers, fleet and host, plus `Form`/`Grant` in accounts. Roots stitch the copies together with 9 `new(..).expect("same source name grammar")` sites and a test whose only job is keeping two constants equal. The copies have already drifted. Fix: one link-values leaf crate (rename `jig-domain-workers`) that the hub may depend on. **Win:** about 445 lines, about 20 types (A5 F1, A0 F10, A4 F15).
- **The Authority shape is hand-walked seven times** across four vocabularies. `pattern_to_authority` is defined identically twice, and `authority_to_authority` actually converts people→authority (A2 F2, A4 F8).
- **The operation vocabulary is written four ways in tasks** (`Control`, `Operation`, `Procedure*`, `Event::Amend/Move/Message`). Procedures apply theirs through made-up `ReplyTo(u64::MAX - 1)` tokens, which turns the compiler's at-most-once proof into output filtering (A3 F3).
- **Several things are written per crate that belong in skein-lib:**

  | Mechanism | Copies | Fix |
  |---|---|---|
  | Bounded name | 4 | fixed-capacity name type |
  | UTF-8 predicate and prefix cuts | 16 exceptions, 8 copies | `bytes::utf8` helpers |
  | `Env { now, wall, limits: env.limits.X }` | 142 | `Env::with` |
  | `List` from items | 24 | `List::from_boxed` |
  | Lossy bounded queues | 33 drop counters | `Lossy<T>` |
  | JSON tokenizer pump | 2 in jig, 4+ in temper | `skein_json::document::parse` |
  | io-owner matchers and hand token namespaces | 3 | `Event::owner()` in skein-io |
  | Calendar parse/format | 2 (+ skein-ui's private one) | `skein_ui::date` |
  | `usize::try_from(..).expect` widening | 444 | widening helper |

  skein-kv's framing constants are also hard-coded in jig in six files (A5 F12, A6 F4, A7 F9, A8, A9 F12, A10 F8, A11 F7, A12 F13).
- **Limits overlap in nearly every area** (hypothesis H8, confirmed everywhere it was checked):
  - the composite core `Limits` keeps copies that are built from one source and stepped with another;
  - the store has three `Limits` and two identical `Levels`;
  - the client has five `Limits`, the view redeclares 13 of the domain's knobs, and the wire schema cites the view (139 times) as the bound's source;
  - ops' sides copy the view's.

  (A1 F7, A2 F3, A6 F3, A8 F4, A9 F13, A12 F18)

### T6. Tests that check a projection instead of the code that ships

- **The client's joint world does not run the clients' component.** `rg jig_protocol_engine_clients tests/client/src` finds nothing. It re-derives core→contract by hand: 15k lines and 154 tests of test code testing test code. This contradicts `client/domain/testing.md` 5, and tech-debt §2's "resolved" (A10 F1, verified).
- **The scripted engine speaks a dialect.** "Most of the client's tests" run on it.
  - It sends 8 read, 3 delivery and 2 outcome variants the engine never produces, and never the `Owner*` documents the engine always sends.
  - It admits documents through a 3.6k-line typed "pricing" that no production code computes; 112 of its 151 tests are about that pricing.
  - Its goldens pin renderings of documents that never arrive.

  (A10 F2–F3)
- **No story runs on two fronts.** The `Front` abstraction exists, but `rg 'F: Front<'` finds only the harness. Stories are rewritten per front (2.8k, 3.3k and 1.0k lines), and some assert what they scripted (A10 F4).
- **The conformance world is not drop-in.** Its `Application` trait has 21 methods and 9 associated types. ops spends about 3.7k adapter lines replaying jig's own core peers and runs none of the 13 shared scenarios. The referee's effect vocabulary is the test connector's, so adopting the kit means compiling jig's test application (A11 F1–F2).
- **Unit tests duplicate worlds and bypass their referees:**
  - about 80 public-API unit tests in tasks;
  - unit tests mirroring world tests one for one in core;
  - fixtures copied byte for byte between tiers in tasks, host and fleet.

  (A2 F10, A3 F14, A5 F4)

**Do:**
- Run the joint world through `jig-protocol-engine-clients` in process, with the client linked.
- Make the scripted engine encode through the generated codec and send only what the engine sends.
- Write stories once, generic over `Front`, and run them on the scripted, joint and protocol fronts.
- Add a `jig_conformance::core` module that answers the core's peers and builds the `Snapshot` from core rows.
- Give the referee its own effect key.

**Win:** about 25k test lines in the client area alone, with confidence going up: the fake gets checked against the engine by construction. About 1.8k lines move from `tests/core` into the conformance kit.

### T7. Untyped continuations and grinding through cases

- **The core's central primitive has no type.** "Child X answered request R, so continue R" is a bare token. It is tried against up to 11 flight maps and three sentinel values (`u64::MAX`, `u64::MAX - 3`, …). That one gap accounts for:
  - 22 token-keyed maps on a 98-field `Core`;
  - a three-bool `tag_tasks`;
  - three wrapper `Event` variants;
  - about 21 `unreachable!` calls;
  - a 1,519-line function.

  Fix: one `Slab<Flight>` whose id is the reply token (A1 F1, F5).
- **The same smell elsewhere:**
  - payload maps that hold four families and re-narrow them with `unreachable!` (ops, the test root, the test service);
  - engine-clients' `Waiting` remove/match/put-back (24 re-inserts, 14 full variant lists);
  - notes' `Kind × Stage` pairs;
  - re-matching an already-routed `people::Ask` with 30-line arms (people, core).

  (A2 F7, A4 F5, A5 F10, A7 F6, A11 F15, A12 F11)
- **Exhaustive lists where a classifier on the type would do:**
  - 1,061 or-pattern lines in people;
  - 414 lines (9% of production) in ops' root;
  - `MessageKind` matched about 25 times in tasks;
  - 16 full `tasks::Stored` lists in core;
  - 30-arm `ReadValue` matches in the client kits.

  Accessors and classifiers on the owning type (`Event::new_work()`, `Stored::activity()`, `fn rules(&MessageKind)`) keep the "a new variant breaks the build in one place" property at a fraction of the size (A1 F15, A3 F8, A4 F5, A10 F9, A12 F12).
- **Event payloads spread as positional arguments.** There are 98 `too_many_arguments` exceptions (51 in core, 14 in tasks). Fix: variants carrying named structs (`Event::Turn(Turn)`) and an owned route context (`Party{request, person, project, role}`) (A1 F6, A3 F6, A5 F18, A2 F18).

### T8. Organisation: god structs and flat piles

- **`Core` is a 98-field struct (37 `pub`) extended by 57 `impl Core` blocks in 41 files.** Its ~45 top-level modules are slices of one object, not primitives. Funding is spread over four files, and file names don't lead to concepts (`goals.rs` holds pools; `memory.rs` holds limit validity). `routing/` is 35 files split by accident, with four `#[path]` renames, while routes also live at the crate top. `documents/` is a read model over Core internals, in a module cycle with `acts` and `reads`. (A1 F8, F11; A2 F1, F4)
- **`jig-client-view` has 107 top-level entries** (the design lists 22). There are near-twin pairs `page/`/`pages/` and `confirm`/`confirmation`, 33 flat `owner_*` files, and 21 phrase vocabularies each listed 4–5 times (11.4k lines, 28% of production). (A9 F1, F3)
- **Other mixed files:** people's `domain.rs` holds five concerns in 2,518 lines; the test root's `lib.rs` is 1,410 lines against ops' 40; core's `lib.rs` holds four concerns. (A4 F4, A11 F3, A1 F17)
- **The file tree and the module tree disagree:** 189 `#[path]` attributes and 40 numbered files. **Worlds are kits:** 9 worlds are dependencies of other packages, and there are 10 dev-dependency cycles. The `testing/` vs `tests/` split no longer predicts anything, and the channel world compiles the reference UI to sign in with an empty script. (A0 F4, A11 F11)

### T9. Naming

- **"historical":**
  - About 1,040 uses in 20 packages, and 0 in the design.
  - It carries three meanings: ended-subject reads, retained results, and old schema versions.
  - It replaced the banned word "legacy". The design says "ended" and "retained".

  (A0 F6, confirmed in A1, A2, A3, A6, A7, A8, A9 and A10)
- **"Owner" means four things in the client:**
  - the envelope owner;
  - a whole-document prefix (137 types);
  - a project role;
  - the view's skein-ui owner, which is the design's term.

  "native" has three or more meanings in the client kits. In tasks, "admission" names four things. (A8 F9, A9 F2, A10 F12, A3 F11)
- **Code-only words and synonym pairs:**
  - "task hub" (the design's hub is `jig-domain-host`);
  - `Reply`/`Answer` and `Stop`/`Cancel` across host's two faces;
  - `account` meaning a source in accounts;
  - `Hold::Effects` raised for things that are not effect failures;
  - 13 public type names defined twice inside core.

  (A0 F7, A5 F2, F6, A4 F10, A3 F11, A2 F6)
- **Crate names don't predict a crate's kind.** `jig-test-*` names both an application and its harnesses, `jig-test-system` is a fake, and the engine's views world is named as a client crate. (A0 F7, A5 F7)
- **Pipeline vocabulary in code and tests:**
  - `qa_f10.rs`, `qa_all/`, `#[ignore = "F: … E-QA-2 …"]`;
  - "decision 1345", "Q39", "QUESTIONS 34" (a file that doesn't exist);
  - "ruling 131", "session 07", "task B";
  - 32 ruling citations in the client schemas;

  The emphasis dialect AGENTS bans ("actual" 558 times and "whole" 537 in the client kits alone) is also widespread. (A0 F12, A8 F14, A10 F12)

### T10. House-rule breaches, and a gap in the gate

- **23 inner `#![allow]` attributes,** against AGENTS §2's "never `#[allow]`". The workspace lint `allow_attributes = "deny"` checks only outer attributes, so they pass the gate. That is a gate gap worth closing, for example with a `tests/boundary` check. (A0 F9, A3 F15, verified)
- **Subset breaches in step code:**
  - `impl Deref` over borrowed structs in tasks (`CompoundTask<'a>`, used by core 24 times; it silently reads unstaged base fields);
  - a trait, generic functions, 8 `Deref` impls and 10 lifetime-parameterised structs in the view;
  - `macro_rules!` in the store;
  - 79 `if let` and 35 `let … else` on jig enums in core (27 of them ending in `unreachable!`);
  - made-up `ReplyTo` tokens in tasks.

  (A1 F12, A3 F3, F7, A6 F15, A9 F7)
- **Lint exception volume:**
  - 795 `#[expect]` in all: 276 `manual_map` (with at least 8 wordings of one reason), 180 `too_many_lines`, 98 `too_many_arguments`;
  - the `[lints]` block copied into 40 test `Cargo.toml` files in 18 variants;
  - `disallowed_* = "allow"` in five kits, which also switches off the replay bans; one kit uses `std::time::Instant`.

  (A0 F9, A11 F12)
- **A personal domain in a public fixture,** at `tests/boundary/src/tests/vocabulary.rs:157`. AGENTS §2 forbids private addresses in public code.
- **The boundary checks are imprecise.** "pull_request", "github" and "gitlab" slip past the vocabulary check, and hits carry no line numbers. Three styles of dependency check miss whole rules; `jig-conformance` linking the test application would fail a rule that should exist. (A11 F13–F14)

The full findings for each area are in the appendix.

- **A1 core (routing, flow, world).** This is traceable, careful routing without a central type for continuations (T7). The root boundary is wider than the design allows (T4). - **A3 tasks.** Real invariants and a strong world (referee, accounting referee, typed coverage, replay). Its accretions:

  - a 2,141-line boundary of 77 events, 43 requests and 44 refusals, with near-duplicate families;

  Comments are often false (for example "`Make` requires this slice empty"). **Top items:** finish the restore contraction, struct payloads, one `Operation`, move the public-API unit tests into the world, reshape the record (a stored format, so the user's call).
- **A4 people, authority, accounts.** Authority's core is exemplary. People is five things in one crate: it carries accounts' vocabulary and authority's stored policy, and 1,061 lines of or-pattern lists. The accounts protocol layer is parts with no component, and the composing code is written three times outside jig. **Top items:** one start path, `jig_protocol_accounts::Component`, sub-enums for `Ask`/`Outcome`, split people's `domain.rs`.
- **A5 small domains.** The cleanest code in jig and the house style at its best. Its main defect is the link vocabulary written three times (T5). Host's `Event`/`Request` restate the agent face variant for variant. Brief breaks the shared shape for no reason, and the brief and notes worlds fall below `testing.md` §2 (no seed, harness or heap check).
- **A6 store.** Sound state machines and boundary, with design-pinned refusal words. The codec is 66% structural and holds domain rules (T4). - **A7 engine protocols.** Disciplined, but heavy. The charter crate mirrors smith and leaves the real translation and its policy to roots (T4). Engine-clients is 14.2k lines with 48 `#[path]`s, and the engine-side translation is hand-written. Config owns other crates' derivation rules. - **A8 client stack.** Good runtime pieces (domain, link component, HTTP, shells) and the worst economy in the tree. An undocumented "unlinked" mode shares the linked client's enums, so the shells panic on its variants. Sign-in and the durability barrier are each split across layers, and `/jig` is hard-coded in the native shell.
- **A9 client view.** Words are cleanly separated from layout, and cards and frames are good primitives. - **A10 client test kits and world.** A sound harness skeleton (`App`/`Front`, a surface-only referee, person stories) carrying two hand-made projections of the engine and no cross-check between them (T6). About 25k lines could go and confidence would rise.
- **A11 engine test kits, test app, conformance.** The harness and fault catalogue are good, but conformance is not drop-in (T6). The test application's root has a different shape from ops' (T2). `jig-test-service` is mostly jig machinery that applications copy, and the boundary checks are imprecise. - **A12 ops.** Careful code, but no longer a template. It is 20.6k production lines plus a 15k world, about half the root is jig wiring, and the root decides policy (T4). The two client vocabularies persist, the family sides re-implement jig-client's watch lifetimes (their retry is dead on the real route), and policy is written as bare numbers. The world depends on jig's test application, so a copier cannot copy it.
