# Records

The families' current versions are the component's 3, the core's 6,
the tasks' 9, the people's 14, the notes' 1, the accounts' 2, the
settings' 1 and the secrets' 1. The tasks family's durable
`MessageKind` holds `FinishRequested { revision }` for a cancel grace
and `NothingAwaited` for a result-ended task with nothing pending
(`../../domain/tasks/lifecycle.md`, 2), and it stores the successful `NoChange`
result and its own result-notice kind, with the producer's bounded
explanation. A live task keeps its reasons and, with its claim, the kinds
its attempt is offered (`../../domain/tasks/README.md`, section 3, and `../../domain/tasks/messages.md`, section 6), each one
`u32` of bits: bit 0 `first`, then `question`, `question_answered`,
`question_ended`, `question_owed`, `escalation`, `proposal`,
`person_stalled`, `result`, `notice`, `nothing_awaited`, `news`, `timer`
and `words` at bits 1 to 13, in that order, and no other bit set; the
offered kinds of a run under a charter with no parts are all fourteen. An
LLM choice, the tasks', the people's and a template's in the settings
family alike, keeps its optional `later`, a main model with its effort and
its sub-agents' models with theirs, and no account (`../../domain/tasks/README.md`,
section 3). The settings family's charter keeps its parts, in order, at
most 8, each its `on` as the same bits and its instructions as text, as
the charter's own are. It stores a job task (`../../domain/tasks/lifecycle.md`, 3.1): its
executor `Job { kind }`, its spec's bytes and deadline beside its words,
its `Job` contract with its result's bound, and its result's bytes; and
the tasks, people and settings families store a job kind among a
delegation's kinds (`../../domain/authority.md`, section 3). The accounts family's source hold includes *moving*
(`../../domain/accounts.md`, section 6). A store at another version of any
family refuses to open under the format check (3).

A `model` setting's value holds no index, so **at open** the store reads the
settings family's `model` changes by key range, before any family's
restore, and takes its names as the given defaults' overlaid with them, so
that a stored vocabulary's model a person added isn't read as gone; and it
takes each later catalogue from the root, before the commit that makes it
(`../../domain/engine/settings.md`, 11).

Settings (`../../domain/engine/settings.md`) are the core's two
settings families (`README.md`, 4.4), under a schema of their own, `settings`, whose
version the format row names beside the others' (3), so that their
records change independently of the core's; and each task's record
and claim carries a revision in the tasks family's schema. From the
baseline (3; temper's `protocol/baseline.md`), a change a reader would
notice brings its patch and moves its family's version in the landing
that makes it; before it, each version kept its number, and its schema,
generated code and one set of golden bytes changed in place (skein's
`data/codec/README.md`, section 5). The settings' version defines every record of 17.7,
charter versions and pending proposals' sets among them. As every
family's: each record stored at the top is
versioned; a bytes field that holds a record or pieces of one says so;
the keys' root type is jig's own and explicit. A store of an earlier
version refuses to open under the format check (3). Before the baseline,
a store from an older build is started afresh when its families change:
its version does not distinguish the changed layout, and nothing is
translated. A deployment made again from the same document,
with no change stored, behaves as it did. Each record of a setting
holds its value whole, a charter's instructions as text; their sizes
follow from the limits that bound each kind (4).

## 1. Codecs

- **One schema per family,** generated by skein's generator (skein's
  `data/codec/README.md`), used by the component only: the core's records, each
  child's, the component's own. The domain's records are translated to
  and from the generated ones with small total functions, exhaustive both
  ways, so a domain change to a durable field breaks a translation's
  build the day it happens.
- **A failed run's immutable activity** keeps its failure class and an
  optional invalid-result kind, independently of the task's current retry
  context (`../../domain/engine/executors.md`, 2). Tasks version 9 carries it,
  added in place before the baseline.
- **A task's ending authority** is carried in the tasks and people
  families, and a template's in the settings family: `EndedBy`'s
  `closer`, a record of the procedure's connector (`u16`) and code
  (`u32`), beside `result`, `requester` and `standing`; and a live task's closer, its
  number, in the tasks family (`../../domain/tasks/lifecycle.md`, 11). Each family
  carries them in place before the baseline, its layout fingerprint
  changing (3).
- **Standing task forms** (`../../domain/tasks/standing.md`, section 7) keep
  `TaskRecord.activation_open: bool` and `TaskRecord.session: Option<Session>`, with `Session { first: u64,
  reasons: u32, last: u64 }`: the first attempt, the choosing reason bits
  and the last wall time in nanoseconds. The tasks family's activity keeps
  `stood { attempt: u64, words: bytes }`, at most 512 bytes, and
  `session_ended { attempt: u64, end: SessionEnd }`. `SessionEnd` is
  `quiet`, `bound`, `unoffered`, `fresh`, `closed` or `rules`. The settings
  family's `Model.cache_life_ms: u32` is the prompt cache life in
  milliseconds, zero for none. These forms are carried in place before
  the first release, their layout fingerprints changing (3).
- **Change results** keep an explicit title beside their words in the
  tasks family. Both fields independently fit the result-byte limit.

- **Each record carries its family's version,** as every generated record
  does (skein's `data/codec/README.md`, section 5), which is what an upgrade will
  need.
- **A collection the domain bounds by its bytes in all is one piece**
  (skein's `data/codec/README.md`, 3), its limit the domain's sum: a verdict's
  fields by the result's bytes, an amendment's lists, an authority's and
  a policy's by the amendment's and the policy's bytes. Only a
  collection the domain bounds by a count of its own is a counted list.
  So a family's limits, made from the domain's (4), are never past
  its ceilings for a count the domain does not have, and a record's
  worst case is the sum, not a count times its largest item.
- **The tasks family's pieces,** each bounded by the domain's sum and
  its items' framing at the domain's count of them: an authority's
  grants, and its note resources, by the authority's bytes
  (`tasks.authority_bytes`, every grant's and scope's segments
  together); a resource's name (a holding's, a writer's, a pool's, a
  hold's source) and an approval's part, their path by the name's bytes
  (`tasks.hold_bytes`); a saved resource's path by the authority's
  bytes; a spec's parameters by the spec's bytes (`tasks.spec_bytes`,
  its words and byte parameters together); a verdict's field rules, and
  a verdict result's and a verdict message's fields, by the result's
  bytes (`tasks.result_bytes`), framed for at most `VERDICT_FIELDS`
  items (`../../domain/tasks/README.md`, section 6), never for as many items as
  the result has bytes; and a batch's creations, a proposal's and a
  verdict's follow-ups, and a recurring template's tasks, by the batch's
  bytes (`tasks.batch_bytes`; `../../domain/tasks/README.md`, 4 and 6), framed
  for as many tasks as those bytes hold. What the domain counts stays a
  counted list at its count: a task's delegates, dependencies and
  holdings, its inbox's items and its questions' lines, a contract's
  choices.
- **No batch inside a batch.** A proposed creation carries its recurring
  template, if it has one, and that template's tasks count in the
  batch's bytes, so the template is within its creation's piece, never
  a batch of its own for each creation; a template's tasks cannot recur.
  So the largest tasks row holds two batches at most, a recurring task's
  template and its pending proposal's, each one piece by the batch's
  bytes, and grows with those bytes, not with the square of the batch's
  count.
- **Account preferences are separate from claims.** People's retained asks
  keep a choice's optional account number, including through amendments and
  proposed batches. Tasks keep that preference and their last claimed account
  separately; the run proof keeps every provider/account/source binding.
- **Authority models retain their selector** (`../../domain/authority.md`,
  section 3). Literal ALL is distinct from a named set, including an
  explicit empty set and a set of all 64 configured models. The people
  family keeps one bounded name context per policy row or retained
  ask. Tagged authority masks reference that context, including nested Create members, Amend, compound operations,
  proposals and ChangePolicy roles. A retained ask's names are unique and sorted by their
  bytes (policy snapshot contexts follow configuration order), at most 64, each at most 4,096 UTF-8 bytes; these retained names
  are priced separately from an authority's other bounded payload.
  Omission is distinct too: Create defaults to the creator's models,
  Amend keeps the target's, an edited role keeps its models and a new
  role takes the project's ceiling. Explicit selectors replace models
  under that ceiling; literal ALL never stands for a frozen named set.
- **Retained request identity survives removal.** A retained ask keeps
  its original canonical selector, never remapped for equality. An exact
  retry returns its retained answer before current model-availability
  admission; another selector on that key is KeyConflict. A new key
  naming an unknown model is refused. Restoring effective policy maps
  names to the current configuration, dropping removed names without an
  error; only literal ALL gains a newly configured model. Answers and
  pending proposals retain their identity and outcome. A policy row
  holds no ceiling: each start restores it under configuration's.
  The tasks family's rows retain only names their own named
  selectors reference, in canonical byte order, with masks reindexed to
  that context. Literal ALL and unrelated variants retain no names.
  Live/ended authorities, pending or decided proposals, recurring members
  and amendment history remap effective masks on load. These are effective
  snapshots; the original keyed ask remains people's.
- **The codec translates; the domains decide.** What a mask or a
  template's choice becomes when its model moves or leaves the
  catalogue, including the index that says it left, and the fixed words
  a bound is sized from, are the owning domain's functions, which the
  codec calls and whose tests pin them.
- **Added dependencies keep when and by whom.** The tasks family's
  rows keep each live task's dependency with whether it was
  named when the task was made or added by an amendment, and for an
  added one its wall and its maker, a party or a task
  (`../../domain/tasks/changes.md`), and those its procedure marked
  started from (`../../domain/connectors.md`, 6.1); a hold for a circle of waits, with
  the circle's tasks, cut at their limit, and when the held task began
  to wait for holds (`../../domain/tasks/holds.md`, 7); and, for a task that left
  its queues and came back, when its wait bound counts from
  (`../../domain/tasks/holds.md`, 2). The tasks family's activity also keeps a
  parked run's `Kept` line, its attempt and pending dependencies
  (`../../domain/tasks/lifecycle.md`, 8; `../../domain/engine/executors.md`), and the
  `Met` notice, the dependency and whether it was removed
  (`../../domain/tasks/messages.md`, 1). The index of holders,
  waiters and dependents is not stored: the restart rebuilds it from
  the live tasks, and judges their waits whole (`../../domain/engine/restart.md`
  ). People's retained answers keep the refusals for added
  dependencies as the client's contract has them
  (`../../client/domain/contract/README.md`, section 3).
- **Question credits share the inbox's list.** The tasks family's
  `tasks-v9` stores `QuestionCredit { number, answerer, asked, line }`:
  the question's number, its answering party and logical addressed
  party (each a `Party`), and the position of its question activity line
  in the asker's record. Words and time are read from that line, never
  copied into the credit. `TaskInterests.inbox` packs credits first and
  unread words after them in one bounded `InboxItem` list, each item a
  `Credit` or a `Word`; decoding separates them into questions and
  unread words. The task record also keeps `asked_requester`, the
  durable latch set by admitting its first question-form message to its
  current requester, never cleared (`../../domain/tasks/lifecycle.md`, 8).
- **Golden bytes** in one set per family, for its current schema, and
  each patch's beside it from the baseline (skein's `data/codec/README.md`, section 5); a fuzz target per
  family's decoder; bounds at their limit and one past it.
- **What jig's records hold, said** (skein's `data/codec/README.md`, section 3),
  each word part of its family's layout (3): the core's
  `TaskBytes`, `PhaseBytes`, `ChangeBytes` and `EndBytes` hold the tasks
  family's `TaskProblem`, `PhaseRecord`, `ChangeRecord` and `EndRecord`,
  and its `NoteBytes` the notes family's `Row`; people's and tasks'
  packed lists say their `pieces`: each pattern's segments, a resource
  name's path and an adoption's options (`Segment`), a delegation's
  kinds (`Executor`), an authority's note resources (`ResourceScope`),
  grants (`Grant`), requirements, permissions, parameters, numbers and
  a note change's references (`Number64`), dependencies, verdicts, and
  a verdict's, a result's and a verdict message's fields (`FieldRule`,
  `ResultField`). A settled call keeps no name: smith's call name is
  its activation, completion and position, which are its key's
  (`CallKey { task, attempt, completion, position }`, the activation the
  attempt). The protocol layer checks that they agree as the call
  settles, refusing one that does not as a malformed call, and encodes
  the name from the key wherever it is sent again (a replay, the
  in-process hub), so neither the core nor the store holds smith's
  codec or its bytes. Live names (the fleet's, the hub's, relays', the
  worker wire's) stay smith's bytes under `call_name_bytes`, and a
  refused call's reply keeps its opaque address, since neither is kept. A word makes a patch reach the field; a patch that
  converts a piece's items, not copies them, is still refused when
  planned (skein's `data/codec/patches.md`, 3; `README.md`, section 11). A settled delivery
  keeps its outcome alone, no evidence: the application that hands
  evidence down at a later assignment keeps it in a family of its own,
  by the call's key, and puts it in the assignment as it makes it
  (`../channel/README.md`, 5.2), so jig keeps none of another owner's bytes.

A settings revision's items, each with its edit and replaced state, cross as
one aggregate byte field of length-prefixed `RevisionItem` pieces, not a
list priced as its count times its largest item. The field's schema ceiling
is 3,221,225,472 bytes; its receiving byte bound derives from the core's
revision bound, including replaced values, and its item-count bound is twice
the settings capacity. A non-exact piece end is malformed; a byte sum or
item count past its derived bound is refused as Bound. The revision's
largest case accounts for a whole set of edits and a whole set of replaced
settings (decided 2026-11-03).

## 2. The application's records

- **Its own codecs,** generated the same way, in its protocol layer: it
  encodes each record and key before the component sees it, and decodes
  each row the component hands back, before its root sees it.
- **Its store version,** one number the application gives the component
  when it makes it. Before the baseline it stayed fixed while its
  encodings changed in place; from it, its patches move it (skein's
  `data/codec/README.md`, section 5).

## 3. The format row

- **What it records:** the version of the component's format (its keys'
  encoding, its frames, its own rows), the version of each of jig's
  families (the accounts' and the settings' among them), the application's store version,
  and the deployment's id.
- **Written in the first commit,** with the deployment's header, by the
  component, and the installation's identifier with it (`README.md`, 4.4), so that a
  crash keeps both rows or neither, and the identifier is durable before
  any sign-in sends it.
- **Checked at open:** a store whose row names another version of
  anything, or another deployment than its header, refuses to open,
  saying which; so does one whose installation's identifier is missing
  or unreadable. Pre-release, nothing is translated (`../README.md`,
  section 7).
- **The levels** (temper's `draft/upgrades.md`, 4.2): beside the
  versions, the row names each owner's patch level, an optional
  number per owner whose records the store keeps: jig's, the
  application's, skein's (its OAuth records in the secrets' store) and
  smith's (its turns' fields, `transcripts.md`, 1). They are fields of the component's
  `Format`, added to `component-v3.schema` in place, its generated code
  and goldens made again: no version moved before the baseline
  (temper's `draft/upgrades.md`, 4.1), and the layouts tell an older
  build's store apart (`Older::Layout`). None is an owner before its
  baseline: a build whose shell hands no set for it writes none. A level is
  its owner's count of landed patches from its baseline. The row is the
  table of applied patches: the levels and the versions they imply.
- **The baseline** (temper's `protocol/baseline.md`): the store temper's
  `main` wrote at `bc23255eb7`, before any shell handed a set, so its
  row's levels are all none. Its row's component and jig families'
  versions (component 3, core 6, tasks 9, people 14, notes 1, accounts 2,
  settings 1) and its layouts' row are the component's constant
  `BASELINE`, written once from that build's `format::current` and
  `LAYOUTS` and never regenerated: a later build's own layouts move, the
  baseline's never do. temper's golden store, made by that build, proves
  it (temper's `protocol/baseline.md`, 6).
  - **Written** by the first commit, with the binary's levels, and by
    patching's last commit (`opening.md`, 2), with the levels patched to; by no
    other commit.
  - **Read first,** by the kept text of the component's version the row
    names (skein's `data/codec/patches.md`), so a store behind reads its own
    row; before the component's baseline, by its current codec.
  - **Compared at open,** per owner, after the component's version and
    with the families' versions, before the header and the deployment
    (`opening.md`, step 2):
    - first, a store at the baseline: a row whose levels are all none,
      whose component and jig families' versions are `BASELINE`'s, and
      whose layouts' row equals `BASELINE`'s, reads as level 0 for each
      owner whose level the binary sets; an owner the binary leaves none
      stays none. The comparison below goes on with those levels. So a
      store the baseline's build wrote opens under every later build,
      patched where its owners are behind, and a build that hands no
      set still opens it as before;
    - both none: the versions and the layouts decide, as before the
      baseline;
    - first, any owner's store level past the binary's: `Ahead { owner,
      found: Some(level), wanted }`, since no patch runs backwards (a
      level is past none); the first in `Owner`'s order, upstream first;
    - then any owner's store level none where the binary's is set:
      `OlderBuild` with `Older::Unleveled`, including another owner's
      none in a row whose jig level is set: a store from before the
      baseline, or not at it (temper's `draft/upgrades.md`, 4.1);
    - then any owner's store level behind: the patch path (`opening.md`, 2).
      Each of jig's families' versions must be its version at the
      store's jig level: `BASELINE`'s, moved by each block of jig's
      patches up to that level (jig's patch 1 moves `tasks` 9 to 10 and
      `settings` 1 to 2, temper's `protocol/baseline.md`, 9); a family
      no patch up to that level moves keeps `BASELINE`'s. Another
      version refuses `Format` naming the family, the version found and
      the version at that level. Jig's patches rewrite each to the
      binary's in the sealing commit; the layouts are not compared here,
      the levels and the versions naming them. The application's
      version is not compared: its patches rewrite it to the binary's
      in the sealing commit. The deployment and the header's commits
      are checked as before; both version checks, the row's own and
      the header's deployment's, follow this rule;
    - equal: the versions must be the binary's, or `OlderBuild`. The
      layouts are compared only where both levels are none: from the
      baseline on, the levels and the versions name the layouts, and a
      change every old value survives byte for byte (an appended
      variant, a widened bound) moves a fingerprint and neither
      (skein's `data/codec/diff.md`, 3), so a store at equal levels
      opens under it. The layouts' row is still written with the
      levels, and read for the baseline above.

    Every refusal comes before anything is written but skein-kv's
    recovery (`opening.md`, 1). A row at a component version past the binary's
    is a later jig's, since from the baseline the component's version
    moves only by jig's patches: `Ahead { owner: Jig, found: None,
    wanted }`, its levels unread.
- **The wires,** in a row of their own beside it (`Wires`, the
  component's): the versions the engine that last started on the store
  speaks to its workers, so that the next binary can say before it
  starts whether they may stay (temper's `draft/upgrades.md`, section 9):
  jig's channel's (`../channel/README.md`, 4.1), smith's charters' and turns'
  (smith's `protocol/README.md`, section 8), and each the application
  names, temper's payload version among them, each a name of at most 32
  bytes and a `u32`, at most 16, handed in by the shell as data
  (`Store::wires`, beside `patch_sets`), so the component names no
  owner's constant. Every start writes it in a commit of its own at its
  switch step (`../../domain/engine/restart.md`), once the store is
  active, after a pending generation's switch, and before anything
  leaves the process: no secret is served (`opening.md`, step 4), no run
  adopted, no connector reaches its system until it is durable. So a
  start that went past its restores leaves its wires, and over a
  patched generation that commit is the first past the manifest's
  position (`opening.md`, 2, step 6), which ends going back (`opening.md`, 5). Read by the open,
  never compared: a worker's wire is checked at its link's opening, as
  ever (`../channel/README.md`, 4.1), and the row is for the tool that rehearses
  an upgrade (temper's `draft/upgrades.md`, 3.1), which says it cannot
  tell where a store has none. A store of a build before the row gains
  it at its next start, and no open refuses for it.
- **The layouts,** in a row of their own beside it (`Layouts`, the
  component's): each of jig's families' fingerprint, and each family of
  an upstream owner's that the store keeps (skein-oauth's `SavedToken`
  in the secrets' sources, smith-transcript's `Turn` in frames), since a
  holder's fingerprint names what it holds and not its layout: the digest of its
  layout, computed from the schemas built in and committed beside their
  generated codecs, which the drift test checks against them, so a
  build holds them as constants. Written with the
  levels, by the first commit and patching's last. Where both levels
  are none (above), a store with no such
  row is `Older::Unrecorded`, and one whose fingerprint of a family is
  not the binary's at its version `Older::Layout(family)`, the first
  in `Family`'s order. Both refuse before the root starts, and the
  store is left as it is (`README.md`, section 2); the refusal's text names the
  directory. `Unrecorded`'s:

  > `<deployment>` holds a store from an older build, made before its
  > families' layouts were recorded, and this start has changed nothing
  > in it. A store from before the baseline is not translated, so every
  > start refuses this one until it is moved. To start afresh, stop the
  > service, move `active`, `generations/` and `transcripts/` out of
  > `<deployment>` together, and start again: nothing is deleted, and
  > the next start makes a new, empty store. The new store has no
  > tasks, sign-ins, accounts or keys, and every client signs in again.

  `Layout`'s, for the settings family (the others name theirs):

  > `<deployment>` holds a store from an older build, whose settings
  > family's layout differs from this build's, and this start has
  > changed nothing in it. A store from before the baseline is not
  > translated, so every start refuses this one until it is moved. To
  > start afresh, stop the service, move `active`, `generations/` and
  > `transcripts/` out of `<deployment>` together, and start again:
  > nothing is deleted, and the next start makes a new, empty store.
  > The new store has no tasks, sign-ins, accounts or keys, and every
  > client signs in again.

## 4. Sizes

`jig_protocol_store::sizes::frame(domain, transcript_bytes) -> Option<u32>`
returns the largest complete current TurnRecord frame for an admitted
ordinary transcript byte bound. It takes the codec's limits with that
field's bound changed, the generated record sizing, and the frame
wrapper the encoder shares. A bound past the codec's ceiling or an
arithmetic overflow is refused, never clamped. Applications deriving
page room combine it with the checked map key-plus-value bound; row,
queue, read-slot and decoder ceilings are separate, and this bound
does not grow them.

The store's `Limits.commits` is its commit edge,
`sizes::Commits { in_flight, writes }`: the commits it queues at once
and the writes in one commit. `sizes::check_commits` computes, from
that edge, the domain's limits and the application's, the largest
encoded key, value and commit, and refuses to start when skein-kv's
limits are smaller (its key, value, operations, commit, page and queued
commits and bytes; skein's `data/kv.md`, section 7), so a commit the root
admitted within that edge is never refused as too large.

- **The largest commit is what it can leave in the map,** not its
  writes times the largest row: each key a commit puts is in the map
  once it is applied, at most skein-kv's budget in all, since a commit
  writes each key once (`README.md`, section 5) and the fill keeps the map within
  its budget (`README.md`, 6.2). So the largest commit is the map's budget, plus,
  for each operation the store's `commits.writes` allows, a key and its
  framing.
  A deployment's commit then fits skein-kv's `u32` whenever its map's
  budget does.
- **The largest value is the largest tasks row,** for any limits that
  make no larger settings revision or application value: a task's
  definition, with its recurring template, its pending proposal, each a
  batch by the batch's bytes (1), its inbox of the inbox's messages
  and two, each item at the larger of a message's and a result's bytes
  with a verdict message's fields, its questions' lines and replies, and
  its ending's result and reason. Its bound is the sum of its fields'
  bounds, so it grows with each limit, never with a product of two. A
  profile's value is that bound, its page and chunk that value and the
  largest key, and its page holds besides the largest TurnRecord frame
  its transcript bound admits (`transcripts.md`, 1); `check_commits` names what the
  value needs when a profile's is short.
